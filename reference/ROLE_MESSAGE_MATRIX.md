
# Role Message Matrix

## Purpose

Use this file as a quick-reference guide for adapting Frost & Sullivan Best Practices Recognition messaging by job role.

This file does not replace:

- knowledge/09_PERSONAS.md
- knowledge/24_ROLE_IDENTIFICATION.md

For detailed persona guidance, use knowledge/09_PERSONAS.md.

For determining whether someone is the appropriate contact, use knowledge/24_ROLE_IDENTIFICATION.md.

This matrix does not determine personality, authority, or meeting type by job title alone.

## Core Rule

Adapt the relevance of the message to the person's responsibilities.

Do not stereotype personality by role.

## CEO, President, or Founder

### Most Relevant Themes

- company strategy
- market leadership
- growth
- differentiation
- customer value
- reputation
- independent validation

### Messaging Style

Keep it:

- concise
- strategic
- relevant
- outcome-oriented

### Avoid

- excessive methodology detail
- long program explanations
- operational minutiae

## Strategy

### Common Titles

- Chief Strategy Officer
- VP Strategy
- Head of Strategy
- Corporate Development

### Most Relevant Themes

- competitive position
- market dynamics
- strategic priorities
- growth
- differentiation
- expansion

### Useful Positioning

The research conversation helps the analyst understand where the company sits within the market and what differentiates its strategy.

## Product

### Common Titles

- Chief Product Officer
- VP Product
- Head of Product

### Most Relevant Themes

- product innovation
- differentiation
- customer problems
- roadmap
- customer outcomes
- market impact

### Useful Positioning

The discussion helps the analyst understand what is different about the company's product approach and why that matters to customers.

## Innovation or Technology

### Common Titles

- Chief Innovation Officer
- CTO
- VP Innovation
- VP Engineering
- Head of R&D

### Most Relevant Themes

- technical innovation
- emerging technology
- implementation
- differentiation
- market impact
- customer value

### Avoid

Assuming technical innovation automatically proves market leadership.

## Marketing

### Common Titles

- Chief Marketing Officer
- VP Marketing
- Head of Marketing

### Most Relevant Themes

- market positioning
- differentiation
- customer value
- external credibility
- recognition coordination
- independent validation

### Avoid

Turning the conversation into a promotional pitch.

## Communications or PR

### Most Relevant Themes

- internal coordination
- executive participation
- external messaging
- recognition process
- public-facing implications
- appropriate information flow

### Useful Positioning

"We're happy to coordinate through your communications process and make sure the appropriate internal people are involved."

## Analyst Relations

### Most Relevant Themes

- analyst identity
- research scope
- methodology
- participants
- confidentiality
- briefing coordination
- timing
- process

### Useful Positioning

"I'm happy to work through your Analyst Relations process and coordinate the appropriate participants."

### Important Rule

Treat Analyst Relations as a partner, not an obstacle.

## Customer Success or Customer Experience

### Most Relevant Themes

- customer outcomes
- adoption
- customer value
- retention
- experience
- measurable impact

Use when these areas are relevant to the evaluation.

## Business Unit Leadership

### Most Relevant Themes

- business performance
- market strategy
- growth
- product direction
- customer value
- segment differentiation

## Gatekeeper or Executive Assistant

### Primary Objective

Do not attempt to conduct the evaluation discussion.

Focus on:

- clear research context
- why the target contact is relevant
- correct routing
- scheduling
- referral

### Useful Language

"I'm trying to connect with the person who can speak most confidently about strategy, innovation, growth, customer value, or market positioning."

## Wrong Contact

If the person does not own the relevant areas:

"Who would you recommend I speak with?"

Possible routing categories:

- Strategy
- Product
- Innovation
- Executive Leadership
- Marketing
- Communications
- Analyst Relations
- Customer Experience

## Meeting Type

Role alone does not determine meeting type.

Familiarity, context, and engagement also matter.

| Role | Typical Initial Path |
|---|---|
| CEO unfamiliar with BPR | 15-to-20-minute overview |
| CEO familiar with BPR | Overview or briefing |
| Strategy | Overview or briefing |
| Product | Overview, then briefing where appropriate |
| Innovation | Overview, then briefing where appropriate |
| Analyst Relations | Process coordination |
| PR or Communications | Coordination and routing |
| Gatekeeper | Referral or scheduling |
| Previous participant | Determine familiarity first |

## Multi-Person Briefings

Useful combinations may include:

- Strategy + Product
- Product + Innovation
- Executive + Analyst Relations
- Strategy + Customer Success
- Communications + Business Leader

Do not assume one person must answer every evaluation question.
