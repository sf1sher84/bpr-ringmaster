
# Objection Response Matrix

## Purpose

Use this file as a quick-reference index for common Frost & Sullivan Best Practices Recognition objections and questions.

This file does not replace:

- knowledge/10_OBJECTION_HANDLING.md
- knowledge/11_SOFT_NO_RECOVERY.md
- knowledge/12_FAQ.md
- knowledge/26_CONVERSATION_BRANCHES.md
- reference/FACTS_REQUIRING_CONFIRMATION.md

The full approved response logic belongs in the Knowledge files.

This matrix helps identify:

- the type of concern
- the response direction
- what to emphasize
- what to avoid

## Core Rule

Not every question is an objection.

If the prospect asks a straightforward factual question and the answer is confirmed, answer it directly.

Use objection-handling structure only when resistance or hesitation is actually present.

## Response Framework

When appropriate:

Acknowledge
→ Clarify
→ Reassure
→ Explain
→ Next Step

## Quick Reference Matrix

| Prospect Statement | Main Concern | Response Direction | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Is this a sales call?" | Commercial motive | Explain the research-led context and analyst project | Defensive sales language |
| "Is this pay-to-play?" | Credibility | Explain research origin and separation from later commercial activity | Unsupported claims about fees or licensing |
| "Is there a fee?" | Cost | Use only confirmed evaluation-stage fee information | Guessing about pricing or licensing |
| "Send me an email." | Time or low commitment | Agree, keep it concise, include useful context and Bookings | Fighting the request |
| "I don't have time." | Time | Clarify that the full conversation is not expected during the cold call | Pressuring them to stay |
| "Why us?" | Relevance | Use verified research trigger or accurate research context | Invented personalization |
| "We don't need awards." | Perceived promotion | Reframe around research and evaluation | Arguing that awards matter |
| "We already work with Gartner or IDC." | Existing analyst relationship | Explain that this is a separate Frost & Sullivan research project | Criticizing another firm |
| "We can't share confidential information." | Data sensitivity | Respect boundaries and use confirmed confidentiality guidance | Absolute confidentiality promises |
| "Do we need an NDA?" | Legal or process | Confirm the appropriate process | Inventing NDA policy |
| "Send us a deck." | Preference for asynchronous review | Agree where appropriate and explain the value of context from a conversation | Refusing materials |
| "Who nominated us?" | Process | Explain the research-led nomination process where confirmed | Inventing an external nominator |
| "Have we already won?" | Status | Clarify that nomination is not final recognition | Saying yes unless confirmed |
| "What's the category?" | Evaluation detail | State only if confirmed | Guessing |
| "What's the deadline?" | Timing | State only confirmed timing | Fake urgency |
| "Who else are you evaluating?" | Competitive context | Follow approved disclosure policy and focus on [COMPANY] | Naming competitors without authorization |
| "How did you get my name?" | Relevance or sourcing | Answer truthfully based on the actual source | Inventing sourcing |
| "Talk to PR." | Internal process | Work through the company's process | Trying to bypass PR |
| "Talk to Analyst Relations." | Internal process | Coordinate with AR | Treating AR as a blocker |
| "I'm not the right person." | Role fit | Ask for referral | Continuing the pitch |
| "What exactly do you need from us?" | Effort or expectation | Explain the appropriate meeting purpose | Overstating preparation requirements |
| "What's the catch?" | Trust | Explain research origin and process | Prestige-heavy defense |
| "Can you just tell me now?" | Immediate information need | Answer straightforward confirmed facts, then bridge to the meeting | Dodging simple questions |

## Questions Versus Objections

Questions such as:

- Who is the analyst?
- How long is the meeting?
- What is Best Practices Recognition?
- Who should attend?
- Can you send information?

should normally be answered directly when the answer is known.

Do not turn simple information requests into objection-handling exercises.

## Soft No

Examples:

- "I'm not sure."
- "We probably don't have time."
- "Maybe send something."
- "I don't know if this is relevant."

Use:

Acknowledge
→ Clarify
→ Reduce friction
→ Reframe
→ One second ask

See:

knowledge/11_SOFT_NO_RECOVERY.md

## Hard No

Examples:

- "We're not interested."
- "Please don't follow up."
- "We're going to pass."

Do not continue objection handling.

Close professionally.

## Accuracy Check

Before answering anything involving:

- fees
- licensing
- exact criteria
- exact category
- deadlines
- confidentiality
- NDA
- competitor information
- final recognition status
- deliverables
- public announcement

check:

reference/FACTS_REQUIRING_CONFIRMATION.md

If the detail is not confirmed:

"I don't want to give you the wrong information. Let me confirm the exact details rather than guess."
