
# Language to Avoid

Avoid language that makes the outreach sound like:

- a traditional sales pitch,
- an award sale,
- a guaranteed recognition,
- manufactured urgency,
- misleading analyst outreach.

## Do Not Say

"You won an award."

"You've already been selected."

"You've been chosen as the winner."

"This is your award."

"We're giving you an award."

"You were selected as the market leader."

"You are definitely going to win."

"Recognition is guaranteed."

unless the final recognition decision has actually been confirmed and the caller is authorized to communicate it.

## Avoid Pay-to-Play Language

Avoid:

"You have to buy the award."

"You need to purchase the recognition."

"You have to license it to participate."

"The fee is required."

unless those statements are factually and currently correct.

Do not imply that purchasing anything affects the research evaluation.

## Avoid Traditional Sales Discovery Language

Avoid questions such as:

"What's your biggest pain point?"

"What keeps you up at night?"

"What's your budget?"

"What would it take to earn your business?"

"Who is the decision maker?"

"What's your purchasing timeline?"

These questions make the outreach sound like lead generation rather than analyst research.

## Avoid False Urgency

Do not say:

"This is your last chance."

"We need an answer today."

"You have to act now."

"If you don't respond, you'll lose the award."

"Our deadline is Friday."

unless the deadline is real and confirmed.

## Avoid Misrepresenting Analyst Involvement

Do not say:

"[ANALYST_NAME] personally selected you."

"[ANALYST_NAME] asked me to call you."

"[ANALYST_NAME] has already decided you're the winner."

unless those statements are factually true.

## Avoid Misleading Gatekeeper Language

Do not say:

"[EXECUTIVE_NAME] is expecting my call."

"We already have a meeting scheduled."

"They asked me to call."

"It's urgent."

unless true.

## Avoid Unsupported Competitive Claims

Do not say:

"You're the number one company."

"You're outperforming everyone."

"You're the market leader."

"You're ahead of all your competitors."

unless supported by verified research and appropriate to disclose.

## Avoid Unsupported Process Claims

Do not state:

- exact number of evaluation criteria,
- exact recognition category,
- exact deadline,
- exact ranking,
- exact score,
- exact licensing terms,
- exact deliverables,
- exact subscriber count,
- exact publication rights,

unless confirmed for the specific evaluation.

## Avoid Overly Promotional Language

Avoid:

"This is an amazing opportunity."

"This will completely transform your brand."

"You'd be crazy not to participate."

"This is huge exposure."

"This is a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity."

The tone should remain measured and research-led.

## Avoid Over-Explaining

Do not give a long monologue covering:

- Frost & Sullivan,
- Best Practices Recognition,
- the research,
- the evaluation,
- licensing,
- deliverables,

before the prospect has had a chance to speak.

Use questions to create dialogue.

## Avoid Excessive Persistence

Do not repeatedly ask for the meeting after a clear no.

Rule:

One initial meeting ask.

One second ask only after a genuine soft no.

If the prospect declines again, stop asking.

## Avoid Absolute Confidentiality Claims

Do not say:

"Everything is completely confidential."

"Nothing will ever be shared."

"We guarantee confidentiality."

unless the applicable policy supports that statement.

## Avoid Promising Editorial Control

Do not say:

"You'll get to approve everything before it's published."

"You can rewrite the report."

"You have final editorial approval."

unless this has been confirmed.
