
# Escalation and Verification Rules

## Purpose

Use this file to determine when a caller or GPT should answer directly, verify a detail, or defer to the appropriate confirmed internal owner.

This file supports:

- knowledge/20_ACCURACY_GUARDRAILS.md
- knowledge/21_COMMERCIAL_VS_RESEARCH.md
- reference/FACTS_REQUIRING_CONFIRMATION.md

It does not replace them.

## Core Principle

Accuracy is more important than appearing to know every answer.

If a detail is not confirmed, do not guess.

Preferred language:

"I don't want to give you the wrong information. Let me confirm the exact details rather than guess."

## Answer Directly When

The information is:

- clearly established in approved materials
- confirmed for the current evaluation
- within the caller's role
- appropriate to disclose

Examples may include:

- confirmed analyst name
- confirmed research area
- general purpose of the 15-to-20-minute overview
- general purpose of the 60-minute analyst briefing
- approved research-led positioning
- confirmed scheduling process

## Verify Before Answering

Confirm evaluation-specific information involving:

- exact recognition category
- exact evaluation criteria
- exact scoring methodology
- reason for nomination when not already verified
- timeline
- deadlines
- specific deliverables
- licensing
- pricing
- public announcement timing
- NDA requirements
- confidentiality terms
- editorial rights
- competitor disclosure
- exclusivity
- event participation
- final recognition status

## Research or Analyst Questions

Questions involving:

- market conclusions
- analyst findings
- competitive benchmarking
- research interpretation
- evaluation-specific market conclusions
- detailed research methodology

may require confirmation from the appropriate research or analyst owner.

Do not invent analyst conclusions.

## Commercial Questions

Questions involving:

- licensing
- pricing
- promotional rights
- packages
- commercialization

should be answered only from confirmed information.

If the caller does not have the confirmed details:

"That part is separate from the research and evaluation process. I don't want to give you the wrong information, so let me confirm the exact details."

Do not invent commercial structure.

## Legal or Confidentiality Questions

Questions involving:

- NDA terms
- confidentiality agreements
- contractual obligations
- legal commitments
- data-use terms
- rights and obligations

should be handled according to the confirmed Frost & Sullivan process.

Do not invent:

- legal procedures
- approval chains
- confidentiality promises
- internal owners

If uncertain:

"Let me confirm the appropriate process before I give you an answer."

## Public Announcement or Communications Questions

Questions involving:

- press releases
- logo usage
- public announcements
- publication timing
- events
- public-facing claims

should be answered only from confirmed information.

## Competitor Questions

Do not invent or disclose competitor participation.

If the applicable disclosure policy is not known:

"Our focus is on making sure we have an accurate understanding of [COMPANY]. I don't want to give you the wrong information regarding other organizations, so let me confirm what can be shared."

## Correcting a Prior Statement

If the caller realizes something may have been stated incorrectly:

1. correct it promptly
2. do not defend the mistake
3. state what is confirmed
4. verify the uncertain detail

Preferred:

"Let me correct that. I don't want to misstate the specifics, so I'll confirm the exact detail for you."

## Do Not Escalate Simple Questions Unnecessarily

If the answer is clearly known, answer it.

Examples:

- "How long is the introductory conversation?"
- "Who is the analyst?"
- "What is the purpose of the overview?"
- "Can you send me the booking link?"

Do not make straightforward questions feel bureaucratic.

## Verification Decision

Before stating a specific fact, ask:

1. Do I know the answer?
2. Is it confirmed for this company or evaluation?
3. Is it appropriate to disclose?
4. Could an incorrect answer materially mislead the prospect?

If there is meaningful uncertainty, verify rather than guess.

## Internal Routing Rule

Where internal follow-up is required, route the question to the appropriate confirmed internal owner.

Do not invent:

- department ownership
- internal contacts
- approval chains
- legal procedures
- commercial procedures

## Final Rule

A confident correction or verification is stronger than fabricated certainty.
