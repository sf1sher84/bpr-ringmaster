
# Language to Use

Use language that reinforces Frost & Sullivan's research-led positioning.

The tone should be:

- conversational,
- professional,
- concise,
- credible,
- confident,
- non-promotional.

## Opening Language

"Hi [CONTACT_NAME], this is [CALLER_NAME] with Frost & Sullivan. How are you doing today?"

"I'm following up on an email I sent recently. Did you happen to see it?"

"You weren't expecting my call. I was hoping you could help me for a brief moment."

Use the permission-based version when it feels natural.

## Named Analyst Language

"I'm reaching out on behalf of my colleague, [ANALYST_NAME], who's leading research in the [INDUSTRY] space."

"I'm part of the research team supporting that work."

"[ANALYST_NAME] is leading the research project, and I'm helping coordinate the outreach."

"My role is really to give you some context around the process, answer any initial questions, and help coordinate the next conversation with [ANALYST_NAME]."

## Frost & Sullivan Positioning

"Frost & Sullivan is a global growth and market intelligence company."

"Our analysts research industries, follow market developments, and track the companies shaping those markets."

"We've been researching the [INDUSTRY] space, and [COMPANY] came to our attention during that work."

## Familiarity Questions

"Are you familiar with Frost & Sullivan and what we do?"

"Have you worked with Frost & Sullivan before?"

"Are you familiar with our Best Practices Recognition program?"

## Research Relevance

"[COMPANY] came to our attention during [ANALYST_NAME]'s research."

"One of the things that stood out was [VERIFIED_RESEARCH_TRIGGER]."

"Our analysts have been following [VERIFIED_COMPANY_ACHIEVEMENT], which was part of what brought [COMPANY] to their attention."

Use only verified research triggers.

## Best Practices Recognition

"Based on that research, [COMPANY] has been nominated for evaluation through our Best Practices Recognition program."

"It's our way of recognizing companies that are performing well in their respective fields."

"The nomination means the company stood out enough in the research to warrant further evaluation."

"Nomination does not mean the final recognition decision has already been made."

## Third-Party Validation

"One of the things that makes the program meaningful is the independent, third-party validation."

"You can obviously talk about your own successes all day long, but it carries more weight when an outside research organization has looked at the market and recognizes what you're doing too."

"It gives the company an outside perspective validating the strategy, innovation, and customer value it's creating in the market."

## 15–20 Minute Overview

"I know I just threw quite a bit of information at you pretty quickly."

"Rather than try to cover everything on an unexpected call, would you be open to finding 15 to 20 minutes so I can give you the full overview, explain the Best Practices Recognition process, and answer any questions?"

"There's nothing you need to prepare for the initial conversation."

"The first call is really just about giving you the context, explaining the process, and answering your questions."

## 60-Minute Analyst Briefing

"The next step is typically a 60-minute briefing with [ANALYST_NAME]."

"That gives the analyst enough time to understand your strategy, innovation, growth, customer value, and what really differentiates [COMPANY] in the market."

"Since you're already familiar with Frost & Sullivan and the process, we can move directly into a deeper analyst briefing."

## Correct Contact / Referral

"I'm not sure if you're the right person for this, but does your role touch strategy, innovation, product, customer value, or market positioning?"

"Who would you recommend I speak with?"

"Who on your team would be best suited to discuss this?"

"Would you mind sharing their name and email?"

## Gatekeeper Language

"I'm part of the research team supporting [ANALYST_NAME], who's leading research in the [INDUSTRY] market."

"I'm trying to connect with the person who can speak most confidently about the company's strategy, innovation, growth, customer impact, or market positioning."

"Who would you recommend I speak with?"

## Scheduling

"Rather than go back and forth on schedules, I'll send you a Microsoft Bookings link so you can choose whatever date and time works best for you."

"I'll send you a quick email with a Microsoft Bookings link so you can choose the time that's easiest."

"If you'd rather schedule now, would Tuesday or Thursday work better?"

"Morning or afternoon?"

"How about 2:00?"

"Perfect. I'll send the invite now."

## Objection Language

"That makes sense."

"That's a fair question."

"I completely understand."

"Before I let you go, can I ask one quick question?"

"Is it mainly a timing issue, or are you still unsure what the conversation would cover?"

"That's actually one of the things we can cover in the 15-minute overview."

## Send Me an Email

"Absolutely. I'll send you a short overview."

"I'll also include a Microsoft Bookings link so, if it makes sense after you take a look, you can choose whatever time works best for you."

"Just so I send you the most relevant information, are you already familiar with Frost & Sullivan and Best Practices Recognition, or would a little background be helpful?"

## Accuracy Language

"I don't want to give you the wrong information."

"Let me confirm the exact details for this evaluation rather than guess."

"I'd rather verify that before giving you an answer."

"Let me check the current details and follow up with you."

## Confidentiality Language

"We don't expect you to share anything you're not comfortable disclosing."

"We can keep the discussion at a high level."

"Where appropriate, ranges, percentages, or general trends may be enough."

"We can clarify the appropriate confidentiality process before anything sensitive is shared."
