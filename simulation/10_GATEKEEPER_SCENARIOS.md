# Gatekeeper Scenarios

## Purpose

Use these scenarios to practice conversations with:

- executive assistants,
- receptionists,
- office managers,
- coordinators,
- communications contacts,
- other people controlling access to the intended prospect.

The goal is not to "beat" the gatekeeper.

The goal is to:

- establish legitimacy,
- explain the research purpose concisely,
- identify the right contact,
- secure the best path forward.

---

## Gatekeeper Principle

Treat gatekeepers as professionals helping manage access and information.

Do not:

- mislead,
- pressure,
- pretend the executive expects the call,
- pretend the call is personal,
- imply urgency that does not exist.

---

## Scenario 1

### Helpful Executive Assistant

Behavior:

- polite,
- asks what the call is regarding,
- willing to help if the explanation is clear.

Opening:

"May I ask what this is regarding?"

Strong caller behavior:

- identifies Frost & Sullivan,
- names the analyst,
- explains the research,
- explains the brief meeting request.

Possible outcome:

- direct scheduling,
- referral,
- email forwarding.

---

## Scenario 2

### Busy Executive Assistant

Behavior:

- concise,
- limited patience,
- wants the reason immediately.

Opening:

"What's this regarding?"

The caller should keep the explanation under 20 seconds.

---

## Scenario 3

### Protective Gatekeeper

Behavior:

- cautious,
- asks whether the executive requested the call,
- wants details before transferring.

Possible question:

"Is [EXECUTIVE_NAME] expecting your call?"

Correct response:

"Not a scheduled call, no. I'm reaching out related to research being led by [ANALYST_NAME], and I'm trying to find a convenient time rather than catch them unexpectedly."

---

## Scenario 4

### Send It to the General Inbox

Gatekeeper:

"You'll need to send that to info@company.com."

Caller objective:

- accept the process,
- politely ask whether there is a more relevant team or person.

Preferred:

"Absolutely. Since it's tied to the analyst's research, would Strategy, Product, Analyst Relations, or Communications typically be the best group for this?"

---

## Scenario 5

### PR Routing

Gatekeeper:

"Anything like this goes through PR."

Caller should:

- accept,
- ask for the right PR contact,
- explain the research context.

---

## Scenario 6

### Analyst Relations Routing

Gatekeeper:

"You need to go through Analyst Relations."

Caller should:

- treat this as a positive routing outcome,
- obtain the correct contact,
- not attempt to bypass AR.

---

## Scenario 7

### Gatekeeper Wants an Email First

Gatekeeper:

"Send me something and I'll forward it."

Caller should:

- agree,
- keep the email concise,
- ensure it is easy to forward,
- include named analyst and meeting purpose.

---

## Scenario 8

### Gatekeeper Controls Calendar

Behavior:

- willing to schedule if given clear parameters.

Gatekeeper:

"How much time do you need?"

Caller:

"About 15 to 20 minutes for the initial overview."

If appropriate:

"The goal is to explain the research context, what the nomination means, and answer any initial questions."

---

## Scenario 9

### Gatekeeper Asks Who Else Is Involved

Possible question:

"Who from your side will be on the call?"

The caller should answer accurately.

Do not invent participants.

---

## Scenario 10

### Gatekeeper Asks for the Analyst's Name

The caller should provide it directly.

The analyst's name increases credibility when accurate.

---

## Scenario 11

### Gatekeeper Says Executive Does Not Handle Awards

Preferred caller response:

"That makes sense. The outreach is tied to industry research rather than an award application."

"Who would be the best person to speak with about strategy, innovation, growth, customer value, or market positioning?"

---

## Scenario 12

### Gatekeeper Says Executive Is Too Busy

Caller should not argue.

Preferred:

"Understood."

"Would it be easier if I sent over the context and a Microsoft Bookings link so they can choose a convenient time if it makes sense?"

---

## Scenario 13

### Gatekeeper Gives a Referral

The caller should confirm:

- name,
- title,
- email,
- direct line if available,
- permission to mention the gatekeeper.

Preferred:

"Would it be alright if I mention that you pointed me in their direction?"

---

## Scenario 14

### Gatekeeper Does Not Know Who Handles It

Caller may offer role categories.

"Would this normally sit with Strategy, Product, Innovation, Communications, or Analyst Relations?"

Do not interrogate.

---

## Gatekeeper Difficulty Levels

### Level 1

Helpful.

Minimal resistance.

### Level 2

Neutral.

Needs clear explanation.

### Level 3

Busy.

Short answers.

### Level 4

Protective.

Challenges relevance.

### Level 5

Process-driven.

Will only route the caller if the request fits internal protocol.

Higher difficulty should mean stronger process control, not hostility.

---

## Gatekeeper Success Outcomes

A successful gatekeeper call may result in:

- transfer,
- scheduled meeting,
- executive email,
- referral,
- Analyst Relations contact,
- PR contact,
- permission to send information,
- clear routing instructions.

Do not treat transfer as the only success.

---

## Coaching Criteria

Evaluate:

- brevity,
- research positioning,
- analyst credibility,
- honesty,
- respect,
- referral skill,
- process awareness,
- next-step clarity.

Do not reward manipulation.
