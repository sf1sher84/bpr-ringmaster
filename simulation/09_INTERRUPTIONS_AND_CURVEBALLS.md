
# Interruptions and Curveballs

## Purpose

Use interruptions and curveballs to make Frost & Sullivan Best Practices Recognition practice calls feel realistic.

These events should test whether the caller can:

- stay calm,
- stay concise,
- listen,
- answer directly,
- recover,
- maintain the research frame,
- move toward the appropriate next step.

Do not overload every simulation with interruptions.

---

## Core Rule

Introduce curveballs naturally.

They should reflect:

- the prospect's role,
- time pressure,
- skepticism,
- familiarity,
- internal process.

Do not use random hostility.

---

## Curveball 1

### "I only have 30 seconds."

Use early in the call.

The caller should explain:

- analyst,
- research,
- company relevance,
- nomination,
- next step.

The response should be concise.

---

## Curveball 2

### "Is this a sales call?"

The caller should:

- acknowledge,
- explain the research-led context,
- distinguish evaluation from later commercial discussion,
- avoid sounding defensive.

---

## Curveball 3

### "What does this cost?"

The caller should answer accurately.

Preferred:

"There's no fee to participate in the research call or evaluation."

Then separate later commercial or licensing discussions where appropriate.

---

## Curveball 4

### "What's the catch?"

Use with skeptical contacts.

The caller should stay calm and explain:

- why the company surfaced,
- research origin,
- evaluation process.

---

## Curveball 5

### "Why us?"

If a verified trigger exists, the caller may use it.

If not, the caller should not invent a reason.

---

## Curveball 6

### "Who nominated us?"

The caller should explain the research-led process accurately.

Do not invent an external nominator.

---

## Curveball 7

### "Have we already won?"

The caller should clarify:

Nomination  
is not  
final recognition.

---

## Curveball 8

### "Who else are you evaluating?"

The caller should not reveal other companies.

---

## Curveball 9

### "Can you send me the criteria?"

The caller should provide only confirmed resources.

If the exact criteria are not available:

"I'd rather confirm which criteria apply to this evaluation than give you the wrong information."

---

## Curveball 10

### "We don't share proprietary information."

The caller should:

- acknowledge,
- reassure without making unsupported promises,
- explain that high-level information may still be useful.

---

## Curveball 11

### "Do we need an NDA?"

The caller should avoid inventing policy.

Preferred:

"I'd rather confirm the appropriate confidentiality process before anything sensitive is shared."

---

## Curveball 12

### "Send me something."

The caller should:

- agree,
- keep the email concise,
- mention Microsoft Bookings,
- avoid fighting the request.

---

## Curveball 13

### "Send me a deck."

The caller should explain that a deck can help but does not always replace the discussion.

---

## Curveball 14

### "I already work with Gartner."

The caller should explain that Frost & Sullivan's outreach is tied to its own research project.

Do not attack another research firm.

---

## Curveball 15

### "We're not interested in awards."

The caller should reframe around:

- research,
- evaluation,
- accurate understanding of the company.

Do not argue about whether awards matter.

---

## Curveball 16

### "Why should I spend time on this?"

The caller should explain the value of the conversation.

Possible value:

- full context,
- accurate analyst understanding,
- questions answered,
- independent third-party validation where relevant.

---

## Curveball 17

### "Who is the analyst?"

The caller should answer directly.

Do not make this into an objection response.

---

## Curveball 18

### "Did the analyst ask you to call me?"

The caller must answer accurately.

Preferred where applicable:

"I'm part of the research team supporting [ANALYST_NAME]'s project, and my role is to help coordinate the outreach and next conversation."

---

## Curveball 19

### "Where did you get my name?"

The caller should answer truthfully based on the scenario.

Do not invent sourcing.

---

## Curveball 20

### "I'm not the right person."

The caller should move to referral.

---

## Curveball 21

### "Talk to PR."

The caller should work through the company's process.

---

## Curveball 22

### "Talk to Analyst Relations."

Treat AR as a partner.

---

## Curveball 23

### "We need legal involved."

The caller should not resist.

Clarify what the company needs and coordinate appropriately.

---

## Curveball 24

### "Can you email my assistant?"

The caller should accept and coordinate.

---

## Curveball 25

### "I'm walking into a meeting."

The caller should:

- keep the response brief,
- offer email and Microsoft Bookings,
- not continue pitching.

---

## Curveball 26

### Prospect Puts the Caller on Hold

The prospect may return after a brief pause.

The caller should resume naturally.

Do not restart the entire script.

---

## Curveball 27

### Prospect Interrupts Mid-Pitch

The prospect may say:

"Okay, but what exactly are you asking for?"

The caller should answer directly.

---

## Curveball 28

### Prospect Challenges the Meeting Length

"Why do you need 20 minutes?"

The caller should explain the purpose clearly.

If the prospect only has 10 minutes, the caller may accept a shorter initial conversation when appropriate.

Do not misrepresent a 15-to-20-minute overview as a five-minute call.

---

## Curveball 29

### Prospect Asks for a 60-Minute Briefing Immediately

If appropriate, the caller can move forward.

If the contact is unfamiliar with the process, the caller may still clarify what the briefing covers.

---

## Curveball 30

### Prospect Says "Call Me in Six Months"

The caller should establish:

- realistic follow-up timing,
- whether email context should be sent,
- whether Microsoft Bookings should remain available.

---

## Curveball 31

### Prospect Becomes More Interested

The prospect may shift from skeptical to curious if the caller performs well.

The simulation should allow positive movement.

---

## Curveball 32

### Prospect Becomes Less Interested

The prospect may disengage if the caller:

- over-explains,
- dodges questions,
- sounds promotional,
- misrepresents facts,
- pushes too hard.

---

## Curveball 33

### Technical Question

The prospect asks something beyond the caller's confirmed knowledge.

The correct behavior may be:

"I don't want to guess. Let me confirm that."

Reward accuracy.

---

## Curveball 34

### Category Question

If exact category is unavailable, the caller should confirm rather than invent.

---

## Curveball 35

### Deadline Question

Do not create urgency when timing is unknown.

---

## Curveball 36

### Licensing Question

The caller should separate evaluation from later commercial or licensing discussions.

Do not invent terms.

---

## Usage Guidance

For Difficulty 1:

Use zero or one curveball.

For Difficulty 2:

Use one.

For Difficulty 3:

Use one or two.

For Difficulty 4:

Use two or three.

For Difficulty 5:

Use multiple realistic challenges, but do not stack them unnaturally.

---

## Coaching Rule

The caller should not be judged on whether they had a perfect answer to every curveball.

Evaluate:

- composure,
- clarity,
- accuracy,
- listening,
- recovery,
- next-step control.
