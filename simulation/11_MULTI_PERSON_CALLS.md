
# Multi-Person Calls

## Purpose

Use these simulations to practice calls or briefings involving more than one company participant.

Multi-person conversations require the caller to:

- manage different roles,
- keep the discussion organized,
- avoid repeating information unnecessarily,
- respond to different concerns,
- maintain the research frame.

---

## Core Rule

Each participant should have:

- a distinct role,
- a distinct communication style,
- distinct priorities,
- consistent behavior.

Do not make every participant ask the same questions.

---

## Scenario 1

### Executive and Analyst Relations

Participants:

- senior executive,
- Analyst Relations manager.

Executive priorities:

- relevance,
- business value,
- brevity.

AR priorities:

- analyst identity,
- methodology,
- scope,
- process,
- confidentiality.

Caller challenge:

Answer process questions without losing executive-level relevance.

---

## Scenario 2

### Strategy and Product

Participants:

- VP Strategy,
- Chief Product Officer.

Strategy priorities:

- market position,
- growth,
- differentiation.

Product priorities:

- innovation,
- roadmap,
- customer problems,
- technical differentiation.

Caller challenge:

Frame the briefing so both roles see relevance.

---

## Scenario 3

### PR and Executive

Participants:

- Communications or PR lead,
- senior executive.

PR priorities:

- messaging,
- process,
- public implications.

Executive priorities:

- strategic relevance,
- time,
- reason for engagement.

Caller challenge:

Stay research-led and avoid turning the call into a publicity discussion.

---

## Scenario 4

### Analyst Relations and Product

Participants:

- AR manager,
- product leader.

AR may control:

- process,
- logistics,
- materials.

Product leader may provide:

- innovation context,
- customer value,
- differentiation.

Caller challenge:

Respect AR process while engaging the business expert.

---

## Scenario 5

### Strategy, Product, and Customer Success

Participants:

- Strategy,
- Product,
- Customer Success.

Useful for deeper 60-minute briefings.

Topics may include:

- market strategy,
- innovation,
- customer outcomes,
- adoption,
- differentiation.

Caller challenge:

Keep the discussion focused and avoid turning it into a general company presentation.

---

## Scenario 6

### Executive Assistant and Executive

The assistant joins initially to coordinate logistics.

The executive may enter later.

Caller challenge:

Avoid repeating the entire setup unnecessarily.

Example:

"Thanks, [ASSISTANT_NAME]. [EXECUTIVE_NAME], I can give you the short version."

Then summarize the core context.

---

## Scenario 7

### Communications and Analyst Relations

Participants may challenge:

- terminology,
- process,
- confidentiality,
- analyst involvement,
- what information is required.

Caller should remain accurate and organized.

---

## Scenario 8

### Multiple Executives

Possible participants:

- CEO,
- Chief Strategy Officer,
- Chief Product Officer.

Caller challenge:

Do not over-control the conversation.

Allow executives to contribute.

Keep the purpose clear.

---

## Turn Management

The simulated participants should:

- interrupt occasionally,
- respond to each other,
- ask role-specific questions,
- disagree occasionally where realistic,
- avoid chaotic overlap.

The simulation should remain readable.

---

## Caller Responsibilities

The caller should:

- identify who is speaking,
- answer the relevant person's question,
- bring the conversation back to the objective,
- acknowledge multiple concerns,
- avoid repeating long explanations.

---

## Strong Transition Examples

"That's helpful context from the product side."

"[AR_NAME], on the process question, here's how we typically approach it."

"[EXECUTIVE_NAME], from the strategic side, the reason the analyst wanted the broader conversation is to understand what's changed in the market."

---

## Meeting Type

Multi-person calls are especially appropriate for:

- 60-minute analyst briefings,
- AR-coordinated briefings,
- previous recognition recipients,
- companies with complex business structures.

They are less necessary for:

- basic 15-to-20-minute introductory overviews.

---

## Scheduling

For multi-person calls, use:

"I'll send the Microsoft Bookings link so you can choose a time that works best for the appropriate members of your team."

If a coordinator owns scheduling, defer to them.

---

## Simulation Difficulty

### Level 1

Two cooperative participants.

### Level 2

Two participants with different priorities.

### Level 3

Three participants.

### Level 4

One participant is skeptical or process-driven.

### Level 5

Multiple roles with competing concerns, but still realistic.

---

## Coaching Criteria

Evaluate:

- participant awareness,
- clarity,
- call control,
- listening,
- relevance by role,
- ability to avoid repetition,
- accuracy,
- transition skill,
- meeting objective.

Do not reward dominating the conversation.

Strong performance means guiding a useful discussion while allowing the right people to contribute.
