
# BPR Ringmaster User Guide

## Purpose

BPR Ringmaster helps Frost & Sullivan Best Practices Recognition team members practice outbound conversations, follow-up communication, and objection handling in realistic scenarios.

It can also provide structured coaching after practice.

This guide explains how team members can use BPR Ringmaster.

It does not redefine Practice Mode or Coach Mode.

Those behaviors are controlled by:

- PRACTICE_MODE_INSTRUCTIONS.md
- COACH_MODE_INSTRUCTIONS.md

---

# What BPR Ringmaster Can Help You Practice

You can use BPR Ringmaster for:

- cold-call practice
- call openings
- executive conversations
- gatekeeper conversations
- wrong-contact and referral situations
- familiarity branching
- objection handling
- soft-no recovery
- hard-no recognition
- scheduling
- Microsoft Bookings
- 15-to-20-minute overview positioning
- 60-minute analyst briefing positioning
- previous-recipient conversations
- Analyst Relations conversations
- Communications and PR conversations
- email follow-up
- call recovery
- retry drills
- post-call coaching

---

# Starting a Practice Call

Examples:

"Practice a Best Practices Recognition cold call with me."

"Give me a skeptical CEO."

"Give me a busy VP of Strategy."

"Give me a gatekeeper."

"Give me someone who has never heard of Frost & Sullivan."

"Give me a previous recognition recipient."

"Randomize the scenario."

BPR Ringmaster will provide the information you could reasonably know before the call and then begin the roleplay.

---

# What You May See Before the Call

Depending on the scenario, BPR Ringmaster may provide:

- company name
- industry
- prospect name
- prospect title
- analyst name
- research area
- known prior email status
- known Frost & Sullivan relationship
- verified company trigger
- practice objective

It should not reveal the prospect's hidden:

- skepticism
- willingness to schedule
- objection plan
- hidden concern
- meeting threshold
- final outcome

Those should emerge naturally during the conversation.

---

# Beginning the Roleplay

The simulation may begin with:

"Phone ringing..."

Then the prospect will answer.

From that point, speak as though you are on the real call.

The GPT should remain in character until:

- the call ends
- you pause the simulation
- you ask for coaching

---

# Practicing the 15-to-20-Minute Overview Path

Use prompts such as:

"Practice scheduling the 15-to-20-minute overview."

"Give me someone unfamiliar with Best Practices Recognition."

"Practice an introductory BPR call."

This path is especially useful when the contact needs more context.

Your objective is generally to create enough understanding and relevance to schedule a short overview conversation.

---

# Practicing the 60-Minute Analyst Briefing Path

Use:

"Practice scheduling a 60-minute analyst briefing."

"Give me a previous participant who already knows the program."

"Give me a highly engaged Strategy executive."

This path is more appropriate when the contact is already familiar or ready for substantive discussion.

Do not assume every senior executive or previous participant automatically belongs in the 60-minute path.

---

# Practicing Gatekeepers

Use:

"Give me a gatekeeper."

"Give me a difficult executive assistant."

"Give me a gatekeeper who asks whether the executive is expecting my call."

"Give me a gatekeeper who wants everything sent by email."

The objective is not to "beat" the gatekeeper.

Practice:

- concise research context
- accurate analyst positioning
- clear reason for outreach
- routing
- referral
- scheduling

---

# Practicing Wrong-Contact Calls

Use:

"Give me the wrong person."

"Practice getting a referral."

"Give me someone who says another department handles this."

Your objective is to identify the appropriate person rather than continue pitching someone who is not a fit.

A referral can be a successful outcome.

---

# Practicing Objections

Examples:

"Give me someone who thinks this is pay-to-play."

"Give me someone who says they don't have time."

"Give me someone who says send me an email."

"Give me someone worried about confidential information."

"Give me someone who already works with Gartner."

"Give me someone who says they don't care about awards."

The prospect may also raise objections naturally without telling you in advance.

---

# Practicing Soft No Responses

Use:

"Give me a soft no."

Possible responses may include:

"I'm not sure we have time."

"Maybe send me something."

"I don't know if this is relevant."

Practice:

- acknowledging the concern
- clarifying the hesitation
- reducing friction
- reframing the purpose
- making one appropriate second ask

If the prospect declines again, stop.

---

# Practicing Hard No Responses

Use:

"Give me a hard no."

Examples may include:

"We're not interested."

"Please don't follow up."

"We're going to pass."

The objective is to recognize when continued persuasion is inappropriate.

Practice ending the call professionally.

---

# Practicing Microsoft Bookings

Use:

"Practice closing with Microsoft Bookings."

"Give me someone who doesn't have their calendar available."

"Practice sending the booking link after the call."

A natural approach may be:

"I'll send you a quick email with my Microsoft Bookings link so you can choose whatever date and time works best for you."

Bookings should be positioned as a convenience, not as pressure.

---

# Practicing Live Scheduling

Use:

"Practice scheduling the meeting while we're on the phone."

The simulation may allow you to practice:

day
→ morning or afternoon
→ specific time
→ confirmation

---

# Practicing Specific Roles

Examples:

"Give me a CEO."

"Give me a VP of Strategy."

"Give me a Chief Product Officer."

"Give me a CTO."

"Give me a CMO."

"Give me Analyst Relations."

"Give me Communications."

"Give me an executive assistant."

BPR Ringmaster should adapt the business relevance to the role.

It should not stereotype personality based only on title.

---

# Practicing Analyst Relations

Use:

"Give me an Analyst Relations contact."

"Give me AR asking detailed process questions."

"Give me AR asking about confidentiality and methodology."

Practice:

- analyst identity
- research scope
- methodology
- process
- participants
- timing
- confidentiality boundaries
- appropriate coordination

Treat Analyst Relations as a partner in the process.

---

# Practicing Communications or PR

Use:

"Give me a PR contact."

"Give me someone who says all outreach has to go through Communications."

Practice:

- respecting internal process
- keeping the research purpose clear
- identifying the appropriate participants
- avoiding unnecessary pressure

---

# Practicing Previous Recipients

Use:

"Give me a previous recognition recipient."

"Give me someone who has participated before."

Practice:

- acknowledging familiarity
- avoiding unnecessary background
- connecting the conversation to current research
- determining whether an overview or briefing is appropriate
- not assuming prior recognition means current recognition

---

# Practicing Multi-Person Calls

Use:

"Give me a call with Strategy and Product."

"Give me an executive and Analyst Relations."

"Give me Communications and a business leader."

Practice:

- managing multiple participants
- recognizing different priorities
- not repeating the same explanation to everyone
- maintaining call control
- keeping roles distinct

---

# Changing Difficulty

Examples:

"Make this easier."

"Give me difficulty level 3."

"Give me a difficult executive."

"Make the next one harder."

"Make the prospect more skeptical."

Higher difficulty may involve:

- less patience
- stronger process controls
- more detailed questions
- more skepticism
- tighter time constraints

Higher difficulty should not mean unrealistic hostility.

---

# Random Practice

Use:

"Randomize the scenario."

BPR Ringmaster may vary:

- persona
- role
- familiarity
- right-contact status
- skepticism
- time pressure
- objection profile
- scheduling preference
- prior recognition
- process complexity

You should not know every hidden variable in advance.

---

# Pausing a Practice Call

At any time, say:

"Pause."

"Coach me."

"What should I say here?"

"How should I answer that?"

The simulation should pause so you can receive help.

---

# Resuming a Practice Call

After coaching, say:

"Resume."

"Continue the call."

"Go back into character."

BPR Ringmaster should return to the scenario consistently where possible.

---

# Replaying a Difficult Moment

Use:

"Replay that objection."

"Let me try that again."

"Replay the meeting ask."

"Give me the same objection again."

"Retry the gatekeeper."

You do not need to restart the entire call.

BPR Ringmaster can return to the specific moment and let you retry it.

---

# Practicing Call Recovery

Use:

"Give me a scenario where I lose the thread."

"Practice recovering after I over-explain."

"Practice recovering after I sound too salesy."

"Practice correcting an unsupported statement."

"Give me an executive who interrupts me."

The objective is not to make every call perfect.

The objective is to learn how to recover naturally.

---

# Practicing Email Follow-Up

Use:

"Practice a follow-up email."

"Give me a busy executive who said send me an email."

"Review this follow-up email."

"Roleplay how the recipient might respond to this email."

BPR Ringmaster can help evaluate:

- subject line
- recent-call context
- analyst context
- research positioning
- meeting purpose
- meeting duration
- Microsoft Bookings
- tone
- confidence
- conciseness
- accuracy

---

# Reviewing an Email Draft

Paste your draft and ask:

"Review this against the BPR email guidance."

"Make this more conversational."

"Make this more concise."

"Make sure this doesn't overstate the research."

"Make sure the meeting type is correct."

BPR Ringmaster should preserve accurate facts and avoid inventing new ones.

---

# Getting Coaching After a Call

After a simulation, ask:

"Score my call."

"Coach me."

"What did I do well?"

"What weakened the call?"

"What was my biggest mistake?"

"How could I have handled that objection better?"

"What is the one thing I should improve?"

Coaching should be based on what actually happened during the call.

---

# Focused Skill Practice

You can ask for practice in a specific area.

Examples:

"Help me with my opening."

"Practice analyst positioning."

"Help me sound less scripted."

"Practice the Best Practices Recognition explanation."

"Practice objection handling."

"Practice the meeting ask."

"Practice scheduling."

"Practice referrals."

"Practice email follow-up."

"Practice call recovery."

Focused drills may be more useful than repeating a full call when one specific skill needs work.

---

# Asking for a Harder Version

After successfully handling a scenario, say:

"Make that harder."

"Give me a more skeptical version."

"Give me the same objection from a senior executive."

"Add a curveball."

The difficulty should increase realistically.

---

# Accuracy During Practice

BPR Ringmaster should not invent information simply to make a scenario sound more complete.

If information is not confirmed, a strong response may be:

"I don't want to give you the wrong information. Let me confirm the exact details rather than guess."

Accuracy is more important than pretending to know every answer.

---

# Information That Should Not Be Invented

BPR Ringmaster should not invent:

- analyst names
- analyst conclusions
- company achievements
- market share
- revenue
- partnerships
- product launches
- exact evaluation criteria
- exact categories
- deadlines
- competitor participation
- final recognition status
- pricing
- licensing packages
- legal terms
- confidentiality terms

unless those details are supplied or confirmed for the scenario.

---

# What BPR Ringmaster Should Not Reward

The coach should not reward:

- aggression
- misleading gatekeepers
- fake urgency
- excessive persistence
- unsupported claims
- pushing after a hard no
- reading a script mechanically
- booking a meeting with the wrong person

A meeting is not the only measure of a strong call.

---

# Successful Outcomes

Depending on the situation, a successful outcome may include:

- 15-to-20-minute overview scheduled
- 60-minute analyst briefing scheduled
- Microsoft Bookings link accepted
- correct referral obtained
- appropriate internal routing established
- future follow-up date established
- useful email follow-up agreed
- professional handling of a legitimate hard no

---

# Recommended Practice Routine

A useful practice session may look like this:

1. Run one complete call.
2. Review the coaching.
3. Identify the primary weakness.
4. Replay that exact moment.
5. Retry it.
6. Run a second scenario at equal or slightly higher difficulty.
7. End with one skill to focus on during real calls.

---

# Example Practice Session

User:

"Give me a skeptical VP of Strategy."

BPR Ringmaster:

Provides the appropriate pre-call context and begins the roleplay.

User:

Completes the call.

BPR Ringmaster:

Provides coaching and identifies the meeting ask as the primary improvement area.

User:

"Replay the meeting ask."

BPR Ringmaster:

Returns to that moment in character.

User:

Retries the meeting ask.

BPR Ringmaster:

Provides concise coaching on the retry.

---

# Final Principle

BPR Ringmaster is a practice framework, not a script-reading tool.

Use it to become more:

- conversational
- confident
- concise
- accurate
- adaptable
- credible

The objective is to handle real business conversations effectively, not memorize one perfect call.
