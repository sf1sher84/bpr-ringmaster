
# Previous Recognition Recipient Path

## Purpose

Use this path when the company has previously participated in or received Frost & Sullivan Best Practices Recognition.

The caller should not repeat the full introductory explanation unless the contact needs it.

The objective is to acknowledge familiarity and move efficiently toward the current research and analyst conversation.

---

## Core Principle

Previous participation reduces the need for basic program education.

Do not assume:

- the same people are still involved,
- the contact personally knows the process,
- the current evaluation is the same,
- the current category is the same,
- the company will automatically participate again,
- the company will be recognized again.

Treat the current outreach as a new research process.

---

## Opening

Preferred:

"Hi [NAME], this is [CALLER_NAME] with Frost & Sullivan. How are you doing today?"

"I'm reaching out on behalf of my colleague, [ANALYST_NAME], who's leading research in the [INDUSTRY] space."

"I know [COMPANY] has worked with Frost & Sullivan's Best Practices Recognition program before, so I won't spend time giving you the full background."

"[COMPANY] has come to our attention during the current research, and I wanted to connect regarding the next step in the evaluation."

---

## If the Contact Personally Remembers the Program

"Perfect. Then we can skip the introductory overview."

"The more useful next step may be a deeper conversation with [ANALYST_NAME] so the team can understand what's changed within the business and what the company is doing in the market today."

---

## If the Company Participated Before but the Contact Is New

Do not assume personal familiarity.

Use:

"I know [COMPANY] has been involved with Frost & Sullivan previously, although you may not have been part of that process."

"Would it be helpful if I gave you the quick overview before we talk about the current research?"

If yes:

Provide a concise explanation of:

- Frost & Sullivan,
- Best Practices Recognition,
- nomination,
- evaluation process.

---

## 60-Minute Briefing Positioning

Preferred:

"Since the company is already familiar with the process, the next step may be a 60-minute briefing with [ANALYST_NAME]."

"The conversation gives the analyst enough time to understand what's changed since the last time Frost & Sullivan spoke with the company, what you're doing differently, and where you're creating value in the market."

Possible discussion areas:

- company strategy,
- recent innovation,
- product developments,
- growth,
- customer value,
- customer impact,
- market differentiation,
- recent achievements,
- relevant evaluation areas.

---

## Questions for Previous Recipients

Use selectively.

"What's changed most significantly within the company since the last time Frost & Sullivan spoke with the team?"

"What recent achievement would you say best represents where the company is today?"

"Where do you feel the company has differentiated most strongly over the last year?"

"What new customer outcomes or market developments should the analyst understand?"

"What has changed in your strategy since the previous evaluation?"

"Who would be best suited to join the briefing this time around?"

---

## If They Ask Why They Are Being Contacted Again

Preferred:

"[COMPANY] came to our attention during the current research in the [INDUSTRY] space."

"The current evaluation is based on what's happening in the market today, so the goal is to understand what's changed within the business since the previous interaction."

Do not imply automatic re-recognition.

---

## Scheduling

If ready for the analyst briefing:

"Rather than go back and forth on schedules, I'll send you my Microsoft Bookings link so you can choose a time that works best for the appropriate members of your team."

If they prefer to schedule live:

"Would Tuesday or Thursday be easier?"

Then:

"Morning or afternoon?"

Then:

"How about 2:00?"

---

---

## If They Want Another 15-Minute Overview

Do not force the 60-minute briefing.

Use:

"Absolutely. We can start with the shorter overview and make sure you have the full context before deciding on the deeper briefing."

The contact should control the level of commitment.

---

## Accuracy Guardrails

Do not say:

"You won before, so you're likely to win again."

"You've already been selected again."

"We're renewing your recognition."

"This is the same recognition as last time."

unless confirmed.

Do not imply that previous recognition gives the company preferential treatment.

---

## Coaching Rule

Strong previous-recipient outreach should sound:

- efficient,
- informed,
- respectful,
- current,
- research-led.

The goal is to avoid wasting the contact's time while still establishing the current research context.
