
# Call Scenario Branches

## Purpose

Use these scenario branches as flexible conversation frameworks for Frost & Sullivan Best Practices Recognition outbound calls.

The caller should not read these word-for-word.

The goal is to sound:

- conversational,
- confident,
- concise,
- professional,
- research-led.

The caller should adapt based on what the contact says.

---

## Scenario A

### Direct to Executive

### Objective

Secure a 15-to-20-minute introductory conversation to explain:

- Frost & Sullivan,
- the research context,
- Best Practices Recognition,
- what the nomination means,
- the evaluation process,
- questions and next steps.

### Recommended Talk Track

"Hi [NAME], this is [CALLER_NAME] with Frost & Sullivan. How are you doing today?"

Listen.

"Good, glad to hear it."

"I'm actually reaching out on behalf of my colleague, [ANALYST_NAME], who's leading research in the [INDUSTRY] space."

"[COMPANY] came to our attention during that work and has been nominated for evaluation through our Best Practices Recognition program."

"I sent you a brief note earlier and wanted to see if you happened to catch it?"

Listen.

If they did not see the email:

"No problem at all. I know inboxes get pretty crowded."

"The main reason I reached out is to find 15 or 20 minutes so I can give you a quick overview of the research process, explain what the nomination means for [COMPANY], and answer any questions you may have."

"Would it be easier to coordinate that while I have you, or should I send you my Microsoft Bookings link so you can choose whatever time works best?"

### Key Principle

Keep the initial explanation concise.

The cold call is not the place to explain the entire program.

The goal is to establish:

research context  
→ relevance  
→ nomination  
→ meeting value  
→ scheduling

### Accuracy Rule

Avoid:

"Our global research team has been evaluating [COMPANY]"

unless active evaluation has already been confirmed.

Prefer:

"[COMPANY] came to our attention during the research and has been nominated for evaluation."

## Scenario B

### Navigating the Gatekeeper

### Objective

Reach or identify the appropriate executive while giving the gatekeeper enough information to understand the purpose of the call.

### Recommended Talk Track

"Hi [GATEKEEPER_NAME], this is [CALLER_NAME] with Frost & Sullivan. How is your morning going?"

Listen.

"Good, glad to hear it."

"I'm reaching out on behalf of my colleague, [ANALYST_NAME], who's leading research in the [INDUSTRY] space."

"[COMPANY] came to our attention during that work and has been nominated for evaluation through our Best Practices Recognition program."

"I sent some background to [EXECUTIVE_NAME], and I'm trying to coordinate a brief 15-to-20-minute conversation."

"The goal is simply to walk through the research context, explain what the nomination means, and answer any initial questions."

"What's the best way to get that brief conversation coordinated with [EXECUTIVE_NAME]?"

### If the Gatekeeper Says Send an Email

"Absolutely. I'm happy to send that over."

"Would you recommend I send it directly to [EXECUTIVE_NAME], or is there someone in Analyst Relations, Communications, Strategy, Product, or another team who normally handles this type of research outreach?"

### If the Gatekeeper Offers to Forward the Email

"That would be very helpful."

"I'll keep it concise and include a Microsoft Bookings link so they can choose a convenient time without the back-and-forth."

### Gatekeeper Rule

Do not imply:

- the executive is expecting the call,
- the executive requested the call,
- a meeting has already been approved,
- the evaluation is complete,
- the matter is urgent unless a real deadline exists.

## Scenario C

### Prospect Starts Asking Questions on the Spot

### Objective

Answer enough to maintain credibility without accidentally conducting the entire overview during the cold call.

### Recommended Response

"I'm glad you brought that up."

"That's actually one of the reasons we like to set aside a short conversation rather than try to cover everything unexpectedly on the phone."

"The 15-to-20-minute overview gives us enough time to walk through the research context, explain the evaluation process, and answer your questions properly."

"Would you be open to finding a time next week?"

If appropriate:

"I can also send you my Microsoft Bookings link so you can choose whatever time works best."

### Important Rule

Do not avoid reasonable questions just to force a meeting.

If the prospect asks a straightforward question such as:

- Is there a fee?
- Why were we contacted?
- Who is the analyst?
- What is Best Practices Recognition?
- Is this a sales call?

Answer it directly first.

Then bridge naturally into the meeting.

Example:

"There's no fee to participate in the research call or evaluation. The reason for the short overview is really to walk you through the rest of the process and make sure you have the full context."

## Scenario D

### Contact Wants to Schedule Immediately

If the contact has their calendar open:

"Absolutely. Would Tuesday or Thursday be easier?"

Then:

"Morning or afternoon?"

Then:

"How about 2:00?"

Confirm:

"Perfect. I'll send the invite over."

Where appropriate:

"Can you let me know when it comes through?"

## Scenario E

### Contact Prefers Email Scheduling

"Absolutely."

"I'll send you a quick follow-up with the program overview and my Microsoft Bookings link."

"That way you can choose whichever 15-to-20-minute time works best for you without us going back and forth on calendars."

## Scenario F

### Contact Is Already Familiar With Frost & Sullivan

"Perfect. Then I won't spend time giving you the full background."

"I'm reaching out on behalf of [ANALYST_NAME], who's leading the current research in the [INDUSTRY] space."

"[COMPANY] came to our attention during that work and has been nominated for evaluation."

"If you're already comfortable with the Best Practices process, we can determine whether a short overview is even necessary or whether it makes more sense to move directly into the analyst briefing."

---

## Scenario G

### Previous Recognition Recipient

If the company has previously participated in Best Practices Recognition:

"Since you've been through the process before, I won't repeat the basic overview."

"The more useful next step may be a briefing with [ANALYST_NAME] so we can understand what's changed since the last time Frost & Sullivan spoke with the company."

Possible discussion areas include:

- strategy,
- innovation,
- growth,
- customer value,
- differentiation,
- recent developments,
- relevant evaluation areas.

Do not assume:

- the company will participate again,
- the company will receive recognition again,
- the same people remain involved.

---

## Scenario H

### Prospect Says Send Me an Email

"Absolutely."

"Are you already familiar with Frost & Sullivan and Best Practices Recognition, or would a little background be helpful?"

Listen.

Then:

"I'll send over a concise overview and include my Microsoft Bookings link so you can choose whatever time works best if you'd like to continue the conversation."

Do not argue with the request.

## Scenario I

### Prospect Says They Are Busy

"I completely understand."

"I wasn't expecting to have the full conversation right now."

"The reason I called was really just to make the connection and find a better time."

"I can send you my Microsoft Bookings link so you can choose whatever 15-to-20-minute time works best."

---

## Scenario J

### Wrong Contact

"No problem at all."

"Who would you recommend I speak with about the company's strategy, innovation, growth, customer value, or market positioning?"

If helpful:

"Would that normally sit with Strategy, Product, Innovation, Marketing, Communications, or Analyst Relations?"

Try to obtain:

- name,
- title,
- email,
- direct phone if available,
- permission to mention the referrer.

---

## Scenario K

### Soft No

If the prospect remains engaged but declines the meeting:

"Absolutely, I understand."

"Before I let you go, can I ask one quick question?"

"Is it mainly the timing, or are you still unsure what the conversation would cover?"

Listen.

Address the actual concern.

Then make one second ask:

"Would you be open to finding 15 minutes at a better time?"

If they decline again, stop asking.

---

## Scenario L

### Hard No

If the contact clearly declines:

"Understood. I appreciate you taking the call."

Where appropriate:

"Would you prefer that I close the loop here, or would it make sense to reconnect at another point?"

Do not continue pushing for a meeting.

---

## Scenario M

### Prospect Wants PR or Analyst Relations Involved

"Absolutely. We're happy to work through your normal process."

"Would you mind sharing the best contact so I can send them a concise overview and coordinate the next step?"

Treat PR and Analyst Relations as partners rather than obstacles.

---

## Scenario N

### Prospect Asks About Fees or Pay-to-Play

"That's a fair question."

"The outreach starts with our analysts' market research. [COMPANY] came to their attention through work they were already doing in the market."

"There's no fee to participate in the research call or evaluation."

"The evaluation process is separate from any later licensing or promotional discussion."

Only provide more specific commercial information when confirmed.

---

## Scenario O

### Prospect Wants a Deck Instead

"Absolutely. A deck can be helpful."

"The reason we still like to have the conversation is that the analyst usually gets useful context behind the slides that helps explain what's actually driving the results."

"The deck can save time, and the conversation helps make sure we don't miss the story behind the information."

---

## Core Scenario Rule

Listen first.

Then select the appropriate branch.

The framework should guide the conversation without making it sound scripted.
