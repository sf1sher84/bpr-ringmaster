
# Conversation Branches

## Purpose

Use this guide to adapt the call based on what the prospect says.

The caller should not follow one rigid script.

The conversation should branch naturally.

---

## Branch 1

### "Yes, I'm familiar with Frost & Sullivan."

"Perfect. Then I'll keep the background brief."

"I'm reaching out on behalf of [ANALYST_NAME], who's leading research in the [INDUSTRY] space."

"[COMPANY] came to our attention during that work and has been nominated for evaluation through our Best Practices Recognition program."

Then move toward the appropriate next meeting.

---

## Branch 2

### "A little" or "Somewhat."

"Absolutely."

"At a high level, Frost & Sullivan is a global growth and market intelligence company. Our analysts research industries, track market developments, and follow the companies shaping those markets."

"[ANALYST_NAME] is leading research in the [INDUSTRY] space, and [COMPANY] came to our attention during that work."

Then briefly explain the nomination and next step.

---

## Branch 3

### "No, I'm not familiar."

"No problem at all."

"Frost & Sullivan is a global growth and market intelligence company. Our analysts spend their time researching industries, market developments, and the companies making an impact in those markets."

"[ANALYST_NAME] is leading work in the [INDUSTRY] space, and [COMPANY] came to our attention during that research."

"Based on that work, the company has been nominated for evaluation through our Best Practices Recognition program."

Then move toward the 15-to-20-minute overview.

---

## Branch 4

### "I didn't see your email."

"No problem at all. I know inboxes get crowded."

Summarize the reason for outreach in one or two sentences.

Example:

"The main reason I reached out is that [COMPANY] came to our attention during [ANALYST_NAME]'s research and has been nominated for evaluation through our Best Practices Recognition program."

Then:

"I'd like to find 15 to 20 minutes to give you the full context and answer any questions."

Do not read the entire email aloud.

---

## Branch 5

### "Send me an email."

"Absolutely."

Then use one light question if appropriate:

"Are you already familiar with Frost & Sullivan and Best Practices Recognition, or would a little background be helpful?"

Then:

"I'll send over a short overview and include my Microsoft Bookings link so you can choose whatever time works best if you'd like to continue the conversation."

Do not force a meeting.

---

## Branch 6

### "I don't have time."

"I completely understand. I wasn't expecting to have the full conversation right now."

"The reason I called was really just to make the connection and find a better time."

Then:

"I can send you my Microsoft Bookings link so you can choose whatever 15-to-20-minute time works best."

---

## Branch 7

### "Why were we selected?"

If a verified reason exists:

"One of the things that stood out during the research was [VERIFIED_RESEARCH_TRIGGER]."

If no verified reason is available:

"[COMPANY] surfaced during the analyst's research in the market."

"I don't want to overstate the exact reason without confirming the details."

Then:

"That's one of the things we can give you more context around during the overview."

---

## Branch 8

### "Is there a fee?"

"There's no fee to participate in the research call or evaluation."

"The evaluation process is separate from any later licensing or promotional opportunities."

Only provide stronger commercial details if confirmed.

---

## Branch 9

### "Is this pay-to-play?"

"That's a fair question."

"The outreach starts with our analysts' market research. [COMPANY] came to their attention through work they were already doing in the market."

"The evaluation itself is research-led and separate from any later commercial discussion."

Then:

"I'd be happy to walk you through how the process works during the overview."

---

## Branch 10

### "We don't need awards."

"I understand."

"The reason we're reaching out is that the company surfaced during the analyst's market research, not because you applied for an award."

"The initial conversation is really about giving you the context and making sure the analyst has an accurate understanding of the company."

---

## Branch 11

### "We already work with Gartner or IDC."

"Absolutely."

"This isn't intended to replace any existing analyst relationship."

"The outreach is specifically tied to [ANALYST_NAME]'s research and the Best Practices evaluation."

---

## Branch 12

### "We can't share confidential information."

"Absolutely. We don't expect you to share anything you're not comfortable disclosing."

"The conversation can stay at a high level, and where appropriate, ranges, percentages, or general trends may be enough."

Do not promise confidentiality beyond confirmed policy.

---

## Branch 13

### "Do you need an NDA?"

"We can certainly discuss confidentiality requirements before anything sensitive is shared."

"I'd rather confirm the appropriate process than give you the wrong answer."

---

## Branch 14

### "Can we send a deck instead?"

"Absolutely. A deck can be very helpful."

"The reason we still like to have the conversation is that the analyst usually gets useful context behind the slides that helps explain what's actually driving the results."

"The deck can save time, and the conversation helps make sure we don't miss the story behind the information."

---

## Branch 15

### "Who else are you evaluating?"

"We don't disclose the identities of other companies being evaluated."

"Our focus is really on making sure we have an accurate understanding of [COMPANY]."

---

## Branch 16

### "What's the category?"

If confirmed:

State the exact category.

If not:

"I don't want to give you the wrong information. Let me confirm the exact recognition category for this evaluation."

---

## Branch 17

### "What's the deadline?"

If confirmed:

Provide the actual date.

If not:

"I don't want to create false urgency. Let me confirm the current timing for this evaluation."

---

## Branch 18

### "I'm not the right person."

"No problem."

"Who would you recommend I speak with about the company's strategy, innovation, customer value, growth, or market positioning?"

Then obtain referral details.

---

## Branch 19

### "Talk to PR or Analyst Relations."

"Absolutely."

"We're happy to work through your normal process."

"Would you mind sharing the best contact so I can send them a concise overview and coordinate the next step?"

---

## Branch 20

### "Let's revisit this later."

"Absolutely."

"Would it make sense for me to reconnect around [FOLLOW_UP_DATE]?"

Then:

"I can also send you the overview and my Microsoft Bookings link in case your schedule opens up before then."

---

## Branch 21

### Prospect Starts Asking Detailed Questions

Do not dodge reasonable questions.

Answer straightforward factual questions first.

Then bridge:

"I'm glad you brought that up."

"That's exactly the type of thing we can walk through properly during the 15-to-20-minute overview."

Avoid using the meeting as an excuse not to answer something simple.

---

## Branch 22

### Prospect Is Ready to Schedule

If they want to schedule live:

"Would Tuesday or Thursday work better?"

Then:

"Morning or afternoon?"

Then:

"How about 2:00?"

If they prefer email:

"I'll send you my Microsoft Bookings link so you can choose whatever time works best."

---

## Branch 23

### Prospect Is Already Familiar With Best Practices Recognition

"Perfect. Then there's no need for me to repeat the basic program overview."

"The current outreach is tied to [ANALYST_NAME]'s research in the [INDUSTRY] space."

Then determine whether the next step should be:

- short context call,
- 60-minute analyst briefing.

---

## Branch 24

### Previous Recognition Recipient

"Since [COMPANY] has been through the process before, I won't repeat the basic background."

"The more useful conversation may be around what's changed within the business and what the analyst should understand about the company today."

Then move toward the appropriate briefing.

---

## Branch 25

### "What exactly do you want to discuss?"

For the overview:

"The first conversation is really about giving you the research context, explaining the Best Practices Recognition process, and answering any questions."

For the briefing:

"The deeper briefing focuses on areas such as strategy, innovation, growth, customer value, differentiation, and recent market developments."

---

## Branch 26

### "What do we need to prepare?"

For the introductory overview:

"Nothing formal."

"The initial conversation is really just to give you the context and answer questions."

For a 60-minute briefing:

"We can send an agenda in advance so the appropriate people know what areas the analyst would like to cover."

---

## Branch 27

### "How did you get my name?"

Answer truthfully based on the actual source.

Then:

"Your role looked relevant to the areas the research team is evaluating."

"If there's someone better suited, I'm happy to connect with them instead."

Do not invent the source.

---

## Branch 28

### Soft No

If the person remains engaged:

"Absolutely, I understand."

"Before I let you go, can I ask one quick question?"

"Is it mainly a timing issue, or are you still unsure what the conversation would cover?"

Address the concern.

Then make one second ask.

If they say no again, stop.

---

## Branch 29

### Hard No

"Understand. I appreciate your time."

Do not keep asking.

Where appropriate:

"Would you prefer that I close the loop here, or would it make sense to reconnect at another point?"

---

## Core Branching Rule

Listen first.

Then choose the branch.

Do not force the prospect back into a memorized script.

The framework should guide the conversation without making it sound scripted.
