# Frost & Sullivan Best Practices Recognition Email Follow-Up Playbook

Document Type  
Operational Framework

Target Program  
Best Practices Recognition

Primary Objective  
Secure the appropriate next conversation following a brief outbound call.

Typical outcomes:

- 15-to-20-minute introductory overview
- 60-minute analyst briefing
- referral to the appropriate contact
- future follow-up
- Microsoft Bookings scheduling

---

## 1. Core Structural Rules

Review every email draft against these rules before sending.

### Lead With the Call Context Immediately

Connect the email directly to the conversation that just happened.

Preferred:

"Thanks again for taking my call earlier."

"As promised, I wanted to send over a little more context."

"Thank you for taking a few minutes to speak with me today."

Avoid opening with generic pleasantries, long corporate introductions, or unrelated background.

The recipient should immediately understand why the email is in their inbox.

### Position the Research as Ongoing

Frame the outreach around active industry research.

Preferred:

"[COMPANY] came to our attention during [ANALYST_NAME]'s research in the [INDUSTRY] space."

"Based on that work, [COMPANY] has been nominated for evaluation through our Best Practices Recognition program."

Avoid language that suggests the research or evaluation is already complete unless confirmed.

### Use the Named Analyst Where Appropriate

The analyst should be positioned as the research lead.

Preferred:

"I'm supporting [ANALYST_NAME], who's leading research in the [INDUSTRY] space."

The caller should be positioned as part of the research team supporting the project.

Do not invent analyst involvement.

### Keep the Focus on Business Relevance

Frame the next conversation around:

- research context,
- industry benchmarking,
- innovation,
- customer value,
- market leadership,
- differentiation,
- strategic growth,
- accurate representation of the company.

Do not make the award itself the main value proposition.

### Keep the Meeting Ask Proportionate

Use a 15-to-20-minute overview for:

- newly contacted companies,
- contacts unfamiliar with Frost & Sullivan,
- contacts unfamiliar with Best Practices Recognition,
- lukewarm or cautious contacts.

Use a 60-minute analyst briefing for:

- previous recognition recipients,
- companies already familiar with the process,
- highly engaged contacts,
- contacts who have already completed the introductory overview,
- companies ready for deeper analyst discussion.

Do not ask a lukewarm contact for 60 minutes if they only agreed to receive a short overview.

### Make Scheduling Low-Friction

Present Microsoft Bookings as a convenience.

Preferred:

"I've included my Microsoft Bookings link below so you can choose whatever time works best for you."

"To make scheduling easy, you can select a convenient 15-to-20-minute time here."

Avoid:

"Book now."

"Claim your spot."

"Reserve your slot."

"Act quickly."

### Vary Sentence Openings

Avoid repetitive structures and obvious automation patterns.

The email should feel natural and written for the recipient.

### Keep the Email Concise

A strong follow-up usually contains:

1. reminder of the call,
2. research context,
3. requested resource,
4. value of the next conversation,
5. Microsoft Bookings link.

Do not try to explain the entire program by email.

## 2. Tone Standard

Every follow-up email should sound:

- conversational,
- confident,
- concise,
- professional,
- natural,
- research-led.

The email should read like a real person continuing a recent conversation.

### Conversational

Prefer:

"Thanks again for taking my call earlier."

"As promised, I wanted to send over a little more context."

"I'd be happy to walk you through the process."

"I've included my Microsoft Bookings link below so you can choose whatever time works best."

Avoid:

"Pursuant to our discussion..."

"Please find attached..."

"We would be delighted to..."

"I am reaching out to formally provide..."

### Confident

Preferred:

"[COMPANY] came to our attention during [ANALYST_NAME]'s research."

"Based on that work, the company has been nominated for evaluation."

"The next step is a short overview so I can give you the full context and answer any questions."

Avoid:

"Sorry to bother you."

"I hope it's okay that I'm following up."

"I know you're probably very busy."

"I just wanted to..."

Confidence should come from:

- the analyst's research,
- clear relevance,
- a credible process,
- a clear next step.

Not from:

- pressure,
- artificial urgency,
- exaggerated praise,
- status claims.

---

## 3. Linguistic Constraints and Formatting

Maintain the precise, analytical voice of an industry research firm.

### Avoid Promotional Language

Do not use:

- marketing clichés,
- exaggerated praise,
- artificial urgency,
- scarcity language,
- promotional hype.

Avoid phrases such as:

"Exciting opportunity"

"Don't miss out"

"Once-in-a-lifetime"

"Exclusive opportunity"

"Act today"

### Word Usage

Avoid using "now" as a filler.

Do not use "growth consulting" or "consulting" when describing Frost & Sullivan in these follow-up emails.

### Sentence Openers

Avoid repeatedly starting sentences or paragraphs with "This."

Example:

Weak:

"This is our evaluation framework."

Better:

"Our evaluation framework outlines the process."

Do not treat one natural use of "This" as a major error. The goal is natural writing, not rigid grammar enforcement.

### Punctuation

Avoid em dashes.

Use commas, periods, or parentheses instead.

Avoid colons in headers if following the preferred house style.

---

## 4. Research Progress Guardrails

Do not overstate where the company is in the process.

Avoid:

"Let's review the research findings."

"Our assessment shows..."

"Here's how [COMPANY] benchmarks against competitors."

"Our analysts determined..."

"You outperform industry standards."

"We've concluded that..."

unless those statements are confirmed, approved for sharing, and appropriate for the specific company.

Prefer:

"research context"

"evaluation process"

"program overview"

"areas being evaluated"

"what brought [COMPANY] to our attention"

"the next step in the evaluation process"

---

## 5. Link Rules

Only describe a resource according to what it actually contains.

Approved variables may include:

[PROGRAM_OVERVIEW_LINK]

[RESEARCH_OVERVIEW_LINK]

[RESEARCH_METHODOLOGY_LINK]

[EVALUATION_FRAMEWORK_LINK]

[RECOGNITION_FRAMEWORK_LINK]

[MICROSOFT_BOOKINGS_LINK]

Do not call a document "evaluation criteria" unless it actually contains the applicable criteria.

Do not call a document "research findings" unless actual findings exist and may be shared.

[MICROSOFT_BOOKINGS_LINK]

[CALLER_NAME]

Do not over-defend the program.

Prioritize:

- transparency,
- research context,
- factual answers,

## 20. Research Trigger Personalization

Where a verified trigger exists, add one sentence.

Examples:

"Our analysts have been following [VERIFIED_RESEARCH_TRIGGER], which was one of the developments that brought [COMPANY] to their attention."

"[COMPANY]'s recent [VERIFIED_COMPANY_ACHIEVEMENT] was one of the developments our team noted during the research."

Only use verified information.

Do not manufacture personalization.

## 23. Language to Avoid

Avoid:

- winner
- you won
- you've already been selected
- final selection
- guaranteed recognition
- award opportunity
- sales opportunity
- lock in the award
- claim your spot
- act now
- last chance

unless factually confirmed and appropriate.

## 24. Claims Requiring Confirmation

Do not automatically write:

"Our findings show..."

"Our analysts determined..."

"You outperform industry standards..."

"You benchmark above competitors..."

"You have been selected..."

"You are included in the final program..."

"Here are the exact criteria used to evaluate you..."

unless those statements have been confirmed.

When details are uncertain, prefer:

- program overview
- evaluation process
- research context
- evaluation framework
- research methodology

---

## 25. Email Length Guidance

### Busy Executive

Target approximately 75 to 125 words.

### Standard Follow-Up

Target approximately 100 to 175 words.

### Analyst Relations or Detailed Process Follow-Up

May be slightly longer when necessary.

The email should not replace the meeting.

Its purpose is to:

remind  
→ establish context  
→ provide requested information  
→ make the next step easy.

## 27. Final Email Quality Check

Before generating or sending an email, confirm:

1. Does the email immediately connect to the phone conversation?
2. Is the named analyst used correctly where appropriate?
3. Is the research described as ongoing where appropriate?
4. Does the email avoid implying final recognition?
5. Is all company-specific information verified?
6. Does the meeting duration match the call context?
7. Is the value of the next conversation clear?
8. Is the Microsoft Bookings link presented as a convenience?
9. Is the email concise?
10. Does the tone sound conversational and confident?
11. Does the email sound research-led rather than promotional?
12. Are all claims supported by confirmed information?
