# Scheduling

Once the person agrees to meet, make scheduling as easy as possible.

## Preferred Scheduling Method: Microsoft Bookings

Where possible, avoid unnecessary back-and-forth by offering to send an email with a Microsoft Bookings link so the contact can select the date and time that works best for them.

Preferred language:

"Perfect. I'll send you a quick email with a Microsoft Bookings link so you can choose whatever date and time works best for you."

Alternative:

"Rather than go back and forth on schedules, I'll send you a booking link and you can pick the time that's easiest for you."

If multiple people may need to attend:

"I'll send over a Microsoft Bookings link so you can choose a time that works best for your team."

Where appropriate:

"I'll send that over now. Can you let me know when it comes through?"

The email should clearly state whether the booking is for:

- a 15–20 minute Best Practices Recognition overview, or
- a 60-minute analyst briefing.

## If They Prefer to Schedule on the Call

Use progressive narrowing.

"Would Tuesday or Thursday work better?"

"Morning or afternoon?"

"How about 2:00?"

"Perfect. I'll send the invite now."

Where appropriate:

"Can you let me know when it comes through?"

## Avoid

Avoid creating unnecessary scheduling friction with:

"When are you free?"

when either:

- a Microsoft Bookings link can allow the contact to choose directly, or
- a simple choice between two dates or times can be offered.

## Scheduling Principle

The goal is to make the next step easy.

Preferred order:

1. Confirm they are open to the meeting.
2. Offer to send the Microsoft Bookings link.
3. Let them choose their preferred date and time.
4. If they prefer to schedule immediately, use progressive narrowing.
5. Confirm the appropriate meeting type and agenda.

## Calendar Agenda

### 15–20 Minute Overview

- Frost & Sullivan
- Why the company surfaced in the research
- Best Practices Recognition
- Evaluation process
- Questions
- Next steps

### 60-Minute Analyst Briefing

- Company update
- Innovation
- Growth
- Customer impact
- Differentiation
- Relevant evaluation areas
