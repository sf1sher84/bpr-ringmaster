
# Scoring Rubric

Use this rubric when assigning scores to practice calls.

The purpose is to create consistent coaching.

## Score 9–10: Excellent

The caller is:

- natural,
- credible,
- concise,
- confident,
- accurate,
- conversational,
- research-led.

The caller:

- explains Frost & Sullivan clearly,
- positions the named analyst correctly,
- establishes relevance,
- handles objections smoothly,
- asks for the meeting at the right time,
- makes scheduling easy,
- avoids unsupported claims.

The call sounds like a real professional conversation rather than a script.

## Score 7–8: Strong

The call is effective overall.

There may be minor issues such as:

- slightly too much explanation,
- missed opportunity to ask a question sooner,
- meeting ask could be stronger,
- scheduling could be easier,
- one or two phrases sound scripted.

The caller still maintains credibility and control.

## Score 5–6: Average

The call is functional but inconsistent.

Possible issues:

- too scripted,
- too long,
- vague research positioning,
- weak relevance,
- unclear analyst role,
- awkward objection handling,
- weak meeting framing,
- weak scheduling.

The prospect may understand the purpose but may not feel strongly compelled to continue.

## Score 3–4: Weak

The caller struggles to establish credibility.

Possible problems:

- confusing explanation,
- overly sales-oriented language,
- weak opening,
- poor listening,
- excessive talking,
- unclear reason for the call,
- poor objection handling,
- pushing too hard,
- missing the meeting ask.

The conversation may feel disorganized or unconvincing.

## Score 1–2: Poor

The caller seriously damages trust.

Examples:

- misleading claims,
- implying the company has already won,
- inventing research findings,
- misrepresenting the analyst's role,
- aggressive persistence,
- ignoring the prospect's questions,
- making unsupported claims about fees, licensing, or evaluation.

## Important Rule

Do not score a call only on whether the prospect booked a meeting.

A caller can handle a difficult conversation very well and still receive a hard no.

A caller can also book a weak meeting through pressure and still receive a low score.

Quality of conversation matters more than the outcome alone.
