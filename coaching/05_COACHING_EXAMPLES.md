
# Coaching Examples

Use these examples to help the GPT distinguish stronger Frost & Sullivan Best Practices Recognition language from weaker language.

## Example 1: Award-Led Opening

### Weak

"I'm calling because your company won an award."

### Better

"I'm reaching out on behalf of [ANALYST NAME], who's leading research in the [INDUSTRY] space. [COMPANY] came to our attention during that work."

### Why

The stronger version begins with the research and does not imply that final recognition has already been decided.

---

## Example 2: Over-Explaining

### Weak

The caller gives a long explanation of Frost & Sullivan, the recognition program, the evaluation process, and the meeting before the prospect speaks.

### Better

"Are you familiar with Frost & Sullivan and what we do?"

Then adjust the explanation based on the answer.

### Why

This creates a two-way conversation and prevents unnecessary explanation.

---

## Example 3: Named Analyst

### Weak

"Our team is doing some research."

### Better

"I'm reaching out on behalf of my colleague, [ANALYST NAME], who's leading research in the [INDUSTRY] space."

### Why

The named analyst provides clearer research context and helps establish credibility.

---

## Example 4: Why the Company Was Contacted

### Weak

"You're one of the top companies in the market."

### Better

"[COMPANY] came to our attention during [ANALYST NAME]'s research."

If a verified reason exists:

"One of the things that stood out was [VERIFIED RESEARCH TRIGGER]."

### Why

The better version avoids unsupported claims.

---

## Example 5: Meeting Ask

### Weak

"Would you like to learn more?"

### Better

"I know I just threw quite a bit of information at you pretty quickly. Would you be open to finding 15 to 20 minutes so I can give you the full overview, explain the process, and answer any questions?"

### Why

The second version clearly explains what the prospect will get from the meeting.

---

## Example 6: Scheduling

### Weak

"When are you free?"

### Better

"Rather than go back and forth on schedules, I'll send you a Microsoft Bookings link so you can choose whatever date and time works best for you."

### Why

This reduces friction and gives the prospect control over scheduling.

---

## Example 7: Scheduling Live

### Weak

"Let me know what works."

### Better

"Would Tuesday or Thursday work better?"

Then:

"Morning or afternoon?"

Then:

"How about 2:00?"

### Why

Progressive narrowing makes scheduling easier.

---

## Example 8: Busy Objection

### Weak

"But this will only take 15 minutes."

### Better

"I completely understand. I wasn't expecting to have the full conversation right now. The reason I called was really just to make the connection and find a better time."

### Why

The caller acknowledges the objection rather than arguing with it.

---

## Example 9: Send Me an Email

### Weak

"Okay, I'll send something over."

### Better

"Absolutely. I'll send you a short overview. I'll also include a Microsoft Bookings link so if it makes sense after you take a look, you can choose whatever time works best for you."

### Why

The caller respects the request while still creating an easy next step.

---

## Example 10: Pay-to-Play Concern

### Weak

"No, it's not pay-to-play."

### Better

"That's a fair question. This actually starts with our analysts' research. [COMPANY] came to their attention through work they were already doing in the market. The evaluation itself is separate from any later licensing or promotional discussion."

### Why

The better version addresses the concern with context rather than sounding defensive.

---

## Example 11: Confidentiality

### Weak

"Everything is confidential."

### Better

"We don't expect you to share anything you're not comfortable disclosing. We can keep the conversation at a high level, and I can confirm any specific confidentiality requirements before sensitive information is discussed."

### Why

The caller avoids making a confidentiality promise that may not be universally true.

---

## Example 12: Soft No

### Weak

"Are you sure? It's only 15 minutes."

### Better

"Absolutely, I understand. Before I let you go, can I ask one quick question? Is it mainly a timing issue, or are you still unsure what the conversation would cover?"

### Why

The caller identifies the real hesitation before making one second ask.

---

## Example 13: Hard No

### Weak

The caller continues asking for a meeting after the prospect clearly declines.

### Better

"Understood. I appreciate your time."

Where appropriate:

"Would it make sense for me to reconnect at a later point, or should I close the loop here?"

### Why

Credibility is more important than forcing a meeting.

---

## Example 14: Wrong Contact

### Weak

"Okay, thanks."

### Better

"No problem. Who would you recommend I speak with about the company's strategy, innovation, customer value, or market positioning?"

### Why

A wrong contact can still produce a valuable referral.

---

## Example 15: Unsupported Information

### Weak

"The deadline is next Friday."

when the caller does not know the actual deadline.

### Better

"I don't want to give you the wrong information. Let me confirm the exact timing for this evaluation rather than guess."

### Why

Accuracy is more important than creating urgency.

---

## Example 16: Research vs. Sales

### Weak

"We'd like to show you the benefits of the award."

### Better

"The reason we're reaching out is that the company surfaced during the analyst's research. The initial conversation is really about giving you the context and making sure the analyst has an accurate understanding of what [COMPANY] is doing."

### Why

The stronger version keeps the outreach research-led.
