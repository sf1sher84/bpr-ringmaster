# Coaching Examples

## Example 1: Award-Led Opening

### Weak
"I'm calling because your company won an award."

### Better
"I'm reaching out on behalf of [ANALYST NAME], who's leading research in the [INDUSTRY] space. [COMPANY] came to our attention during that work."

### Why
The better version leads with research and does not imply a final recognition decision has already been made.

---

## Example 2: Vague Meeting Ask

### Weak
"When are you free?"

### Better
"Would Tuesday or Thursday work better?"

### Why
The second version reduces scheduling friction.

---

## Example 3: Over-Explaining

### Weak
Letting the opening run for several minutes before asking a question.

### Better
"Are you familiar with Frost & Sullivan and what we do?"

### Why
This creates dialogue and lets the caller adjust how much explanation is needed.

---

## Example 4: Busy Objection

### Weak
"But this will only take 15 minutes."

### Better
"I completely understand. I wasn't expecting to have the full conversation right now. The reason I called was really just to find a better time."

### Why
The better version acknowledges the objection without arguing.

---

## Example 5: Soft No

### Weak
Asking for the meeting repeatedly.

### Better
"Before I let you go, can I ask one quick question? Is it mainly a timing issue, or are you still unsure what the conversation would cover?"

### Why
The caller diagnoses the hesitation before making one final meeting ask.

---

## Example 6: Unsupported Claim

### Weak
"You were selected because you're the market leader."

### Better
"[COMPANY] surfaced during the analyst's research, and I don't want to overstate the exact reason without confirming the details."

### Why
Accuracy is more important than sounding persuasive.
