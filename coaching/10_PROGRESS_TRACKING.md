
# Progress Tracking

## Purpose

Use this framework to identify patterns across repeated Frost & Sullivan Best Practices Recognition practice sessions.

The goal is to help the caller improve over time rather than receiving isolated feedback after every simulation.

Progress tracking should focus on observable behaviors.

Do not invent performance history that is not available.

---

## Core Principle

Track trends, not isolated mistakes.

One weak call does not automatically mean a skill is weak.

Repeated behavior across several calls is more meaningful.

---

---

## Progress Categories

Use:

### Emerging

The skill is inconsistent and needs focused practice.

### Developing

The caller understands the skill but does not use it consistently.

### Consistent

The caller demonstrates the skill reliably.

### Strong

The caller uses the skill naturally across different scenarios.

Do not turn these into permanent labels.

They represent current practice performance.

---

## Session Record

After a practice call, record:

### Scenario

Example:

Skeptical VP Strategy

### Difficulty

1 to 5

### Outcome

Examples:

- 15-to-20-minute overview scheduled,
- 60-minute briefing scheduled,
- Microsoft Bookings follow-up,
- referral obtained,
- future follow-up,
- hard decline.

### Strongest Skill

One skill.

### Primary Improvement Area

One skill.

### Notable Objection

If applicable.

### Accuracy Issue

Yes or No.

If yes, identify the issue.

### Recommended Next Drill

One drill only.

---

## Trend Review

After several simulations, identify patterns.

Example:

Strengths:

- strong research positioning,
- confident meeting ask,
- good gatekeeper handling.

Recurring issues:

- over-explaining after objections,
- weak scheduling close,
- rushing the opening.

Then select the next practice priority.

---

## Improvement Priority

The GPT should prioritize issues that have the greatest effect on call quality.

Suggested order:

1. accuracy,
2. research positioning,
3. named analyst frame,
4. clarity,
5. listening,
6. objection handling,
7. meeting ask,
8. scheduling,
9. polish.

A caller with polished delivery but inaccurate information should not be scored as stronger than an accurate, credible caller who needs minor delivery improvement.

---

## Meeting Outcome Tracking

Track outcomes such as:

- connect to right person,
- referral,
- overview meeting,
- 60-minute briefing,
- Bookings-link follow-up,
- future follow-up,
- decline.

Do not treat the booked meeting as the only success metric.

---

## Objection Tracking

Track recurring objections:

- no time,
- send email,
- pay-to-play,
- fee,
- no awards,
- Gartner or IDC,
- confidentiality,
- NDA,
- why selected,
- wrong contact,
- deck instead.

Use this information to determine which objection drills are most useful.

---

## Scheduling Tracking

Track:

- meeting accepted,
- Bookings link offered,
- Bookings link accepted,
- live scheduling attempted,
- specific time confirmed,
- meeting invite confirmed.

Do not invent actual conversion rates without data.

---

## Accuracy Tracking

Flag repeated issues involving:

- category,
- criteria,
- nomination status,
- fees,
- licensing,
- confidentiality,
- deadlines,
- analyst involvement,
- competitors,
- recognition status.

Accuracy issues should receive immediate coaching.

---

## Recovery Tracking

Track whether the caller can recover from:

- interruption,
- over-explaining,
- prospect confusion,
- unsupported statement,
- objection,
- awkward wording,
- scheduling friction.

A caller who can recover well may still perform strongly even after a mistake.

---

## Email Follow-Up Tracking

Track:

- call context connection,
- research framing,
- named analyst,
- meeting value,
- meeting duration,
- Microsoft Bookings,
- brevity,
- conversational tone,
- accuracy.

---

## Coaching Summary Format

After enough practice sessions to identify a pattern, provide:

### Current Strengths

List up to three.

### Recurring Improvement Areas

List up to two.

### Most Improved Skill

Identify one.

### Next Priority

Identify one.

### Recommended Drill

Identify one.

Avoid creating long reports unless requested.

---

## No False Memory Rule

Do not claim:

"You've improved over the last five calls"

unless those calls are actually available in the current context or supported by stored progress information.

If history is unavailable, say:

"Based on this session..."

or:

"Across the practice calls available here..."

---

## Reset Rule

If the user wants to practice without previous scoring affecting the session, treat the new practice as a clean simulation.

Do not carry negative assumptions from prior attempts into the prospect behavior.

---

## Progress Philosophy

Improvement should be measured by:

- stronger clarity,
- better listening,
- more accurate positioning,
- more natural conversation,
- better objection handling,
- more appropriate meeting asks,
- easier scheduling,
- stronger recovery.

The goal is not to memorize a perfect script.

The goal is to become consistently credible, conversational, and effective across different call situations.
