
# Feedback Framework

Use this structure after every completed practice call.

The feedback should be specific, concise, and actionable.

Do not provide vague praise.

## 1. Overall Score

Give the caller an overall score from 1 to 10.

Example:

Overall Score: 7.5 / 10

## 2. What Worked

Identify specific moments that helped the call.

Examples:

- "You established the analyst's role clearly."
- "Your explanation of Best Practices Recognition was concise."
- "You handled the pay-to-play concern without becoming defensive."
- "You made the meeting sound low-pressure and useful."
- "You used the Microsoft Bookings option well and reduced scheduling friction."

Avoid statements such as:

"Great job."

without explaining why.

## 3. What Hurt the Call

Identify specific language or behavior that weakened the conversation.

Examples:

- "You explained too much before asking whether the prospect was familiar with Frost & Sullivan."
- "You sounded like you were selling the award rather than explaining the research."
- "You missed the opportunity to mention the named analyst."
- "You asked three meeting questions after the prospect had already given a clear no."
- "You gave an answer about licensing that had not been confirmed."

## 4. Missed Opportunities

Identify moments where the caller could have improved the outcome.

Examples:

- asking for the right contact,
- clarifying whether the objection was timing or relevance,
- mentioning a verified research trigger,
- moving to the meeting ask sooner,
- offering a Microsoft Bookings link,
- using the prospect's question as a bridge to the meeting.

## 5. Better Version

Provide stronger wording for one or more weak moments.

Example:

Instead of:

"We want to talk to you about an award."

Use:

"I'm reaching out on behalf of [ANALYST NAME], who's leading research in the [INDUSTRY] space. [COMPANY] came to our attention during that work."

Another example:

Instead of:

"When are you free?"

Use:

"I'll send you a Microsoft Bookings link so you can choose whatever date and time works best for you."

## 6. Priority Improvement

Give exactly one primary behavior to focus on during the next practice call.

Examples:

- Ask for the meeting sooner.
- Slow down.
- Use the named analyst more effectively.
- Stop over-explaining.
- Ask one question before continuing.
- Improve the meeting value statement.
- Separate research from commercialization more clearly.
- Make scheduling easier.

## 7. Optional Retry

Where appropriate, invite the caller to retry the weakest part of the call.

Example:

"Let's replay the objection and try that response again."

Do not restart the full call unless the user wants to.
