# Common Caller Mistakes

## Purpose

Use this guide to identify recurring habits that reduce credibility, clarity, or meeting conversion during Frost & Sullivan Best Practices Recognition outreach.

The goal is not to punish mistakes.

The goal is to recognize them quickly and correct them.

---

## 1. Over-Explaining the Program

### What It Sounds Like

The caller spends several minutes describing:

- Frost & Sullivan,
- the award,
- the research,
- the evaluation,
- methodology,
- recognition,
- commercial options,

before allowing the prospect to speak.

### Why It Hurts

The prospect becomes overloaded and may assume the call is a sales pitch.

### Better Approach

Give enough context to establish relevance.

Then create dialogue.

Preferred:

"[COMPANY] came to our attention during [ANALYST_NAME]'s research and has been nominated for evaluation through our Best Practices Recognition program."

Then:

"Are you familiar with Frost & Sullivan and what we do?"

---

## 2. Leading With the Award

### What It Sounds Like

"I'm calling because you've been nominated for an award."

### Why It Hurts

It can sound promotional, generic, or pay-to-play.

### Better Approach

Lead with:

research  
→ analyst  
→ company relevance  
→ nomination

---

## 3. Missing the Named Analyst

### Problem

The caller says:

"Our research team is looking at your company."

without identifying the research lead.

### Why It Hurts

The outreach sounds generic.

### Better Approach

Use:

"I'm reaching out on behalf of [ANALYST_NAME], who's leading research in the [INDUSTRY] space."

---

## 4. Sounding Too Sales-Oriented

### Warning Signs

- "Great opportunity"
- "Exciting opportunity"
- "We'd love to give you an award"
- "I'd love to tell you about our program"
- aggressive urgency
- prestige-heavy language

### Better Approach

Keep the conversation research-led.

Use:

"The company surfaced during the analyst's market research."

---

## 5. Sounding Apologetic

### Warning Signs

- "Sorry to bother you."
- "I know you're super busy."
- "I hope it's okay that I called."
- "I just wanted to..."

### Why It Hurts

It lowers confidence before the caller has explained the relevance.

### Better Approach

Be courteous without apologizing for legitimate outreach.

---

## 6. Talking Too Fast

### Why It Happens

The caller wants to get through the script before being interrupted.

### Why It Hurts

The call sounds rehearsed and creates cognitive overload.

### Better Approach

Slow down.

Use shorter sentences.

Pause after important points.

Let the prospect respond.

---

## 7. Reading the Script Word-for-Word

### Why It Hurts

The caller stops reacting naturally.

### Better Approach

Treat the script as a framework.

Know:

- the objective,
- the research positioning,
- the key transition,
- the meeting ask.

Then speak naturally.

---

## 8. Weak Meeting Ask

### Weak

"Would you maybe be interested in learning more?"

### Why It Hurts

The prospect does not know:

- what the meeting is,
- how long it is,
- why it matters.

### Better

"I'd like to find 15 to 20 minutes so I can give you the full context, walk you through the evaluation process, and answer any questions."

---

## 9. Asking for Too Much Time

### Mistake

Requesting a 60-minute briefing from someone who barely agreed to hear more.

### Better Approach

Use the 15-to-20-minute overview first.

Move to the 60-minute analyst briefing only when appropriate.

---

## 10. Vague Scheduling

### Weak

"Let me know when you're available."

### Better

"I'll send you my Microsoft Bookings link so you can choose whatever time works best."

Or live:

"Would Tuesday or Thursday be easier?"

---

## 11. Fighting Send Me an Email

### Mistake

The caller keeps trying to book after the prospect asks for email.

### Better Approach

Accept the request.

Then make the email easy to act on.

"Absolutely. I'll keep it concise and include my Microsoft Bookings link."

---

## 12. Treating Every Question as an Objection

### Example

Prospect:

"Who is the analyst?"

Caller treats it as resistance.

### Better Approach

Answer questions as questions.

Do not use objection frameworks when simple information is being requested.

---

## 13. Dodging Straightforward Questions

### Mistake

The caller says:

"We can talk about that in the meeting."

to every question.

### Why It Hurts

It creates distrust.

### Better Approach

Answer known factual questions directly.

Then use the meeting for the broader context.

---

## 14. Becoming Defensive

### Warning Signs

- arguing,
- correcting the prospect aggressively,
- overexplaining legitimacy,
- becoming emotionally reactive.

### Better Approach

Use:

"That's a fair question."

Then provide factual context.

---

## 15. Overcoming a Hard No

### Mistake

The caller continues asking after a clear decline.

### Better Approach

Respect the decision.

"Understood. I appreciate your time."

---

## 16. Failing to Clarify a Soft No

### Prospect

"I'm not sure we have time for this."

### Mistake

Immediately ending the call.

### Better Approach

Clarify:

"Is it mainly a timing issue, or are you still unsure what the conversation would cover?"

Then make one appropriate second ask.

---

## 17. Booking the Wrong Person

### Mistake

Treating any meeting as success.

### Better Approach

Confirm that the person can speak to relevant areas.

If not:

"Who would you recommend I speak with?"

---

## 18. Ignoring Gatekeepers

### Mistake

Treating assistants as obstacles.

### Better Approach

Give enough context for them to help.

Ask for guidance.

---

## 19. Inventing Personalization

### Mistake

Using unverified company achievements to sound researched.

### Better Approach

Only use verified triggers.

If none exist, use the accurate research frame.

---

## 20. Overstating the Evaluation

### Warning Signs

- "You won."
- "You've been selected."
- "Our findings show you're the leader."
- "You outperform competitors."

### Better Approach

Use:

"nominated for evaluation"

unless more advanced status is confirmed.

---

## 21. Guessing When Unsure

### Mistake

Inventing answers about:

- category,
- criteria,
- deadlines,
- licensing,
- confidentiality,
- fees,
- competitors.

### Better Approach

"I don't want to give you the wrong information. Let me confirm that."

---

## 22. Failing to Separate Research and Commercial Discussion

### Why It Hurts

It can reinforce pay-to-play concerns.

### Better Approach

Clearly distinguish:

research and evaluation  
from  
later commercial or licensing discussions.

---

## 23. Using Fake Urgency

Avoid:

- "We need to do this today."
- "Availability is closing."
- "We're almost done selecting winners."

unless there is a real confirmed deadline.

---

## 24. Filling Every Silence

### Mistake

The caller keeps talking because the prospect pauses.

### Better Approach

Allow the silence.

The prospect may simply be thinking.

---

## 25. Giving Too Many Objection Answers

### Mistake

The caller answers objections the prospect never raised.

### Why It Hurts

It introduces concerns that did not exist.

### Better Approach

Respond only to the actual concern.

---

# Coaching Rule

When a mistake appears, identify:

1. what happened,
2. why it weakened the call,
3. what to say instead,
4. one behavior to practice next.

Do not overwhelm the caller with every possible correction at once.
