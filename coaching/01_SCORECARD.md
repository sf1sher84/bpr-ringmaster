
# Call Scorecard

Use this scorecard after each Frost & Sullivan Best Practices Recognition practice call.

Score each category from 1 to 10.

The purpose of the scorecard is to evaluate whether the caller handled the conversation in a credible, natural, research-led way.

## 1. Opening

Evaluate whether the caller:

- sounded natural and confident,
- identified themselves clearly,
- kept the opening concise,
- avoided sounding overly scripted,
- established enough context to continue the conversation.

Questions to consider:

- Did the opening feel conversational?
- Did the caller avoid launching into a long monologue?
- Did the caller earn the prospect's attention?

## 2. Research Positioning

Evaluate whether the caller correctly positioned the outreach as research-led.

The caller should communicate that:

- Frost & Sullivan is conducting industry research,
- the company surfaced during that research,
- the outreach is tied to Best Practices Recognition,
- the conversation is not a traditional sales pitch.

Higher scores should be given when the research frame is clear and believable.

## 3. Named Analyst Frame

Evaluate whether the caller correctly positioned the named industry analyst.

Preferred structure:

"I'm reaching out on behalf of my colleague, [ANALYST NAME], who's leading research in the [INDUSTRY] space."

The caller should:

- identify the analyst as the research lead,
- position themselves as supporting the analyst's work,
- avoid overstating the analyst's involvement,
- avoid inventing analyst activity.

## 4. Relevance

Evaluate whether the caller made the outreach feel relevant to the company.

Strong relevance may include:

- a verified company achievement,
- a market development,
- a product launch,
- a strategic partnership,
- growth,
- innovation,
- expansion,
- another verified research trigger.

The caller should never invent a reason for the company being contacted.

## 5. Conversational Tone

Evaluate whether the caller sounded:

- natural,
- relaxed,
- confident,
- professional,
- interested,
- human.

Reduce the score if the caller sounds:

- robotic,
- rushed,
- overly formal,
- overly enthusiastic,
- apologetic,
- sales-heavy.

## 6. Best Practices Recognition Explanation

Evaluate whether the caller clearly explained Best Practices Recognition.

The caller should communicate that:

- the company has been nominated for evaluation,
- nomination does not mean final recognition,
- the program is research-led,
- the purpose is to recognize companies performing well in their fields.

## 7. Independent Third-Party Validation

Evaluate whether the caller effectively communicated the value of independent validation.

Examples:

"One of the things that makes the program meaningful is the independent, third-party validation."

or:

"A company can obviously talk about its own successes, but it carries more weight when an outside research organization has looked at the market and recognizes what it's doing well."

The caller should not sound promotional or exaggerated.

## 8. Listening

Evaluate whether the caller:

- responded to what the prospect actually said,
- avoided interrupting unnecessarily,
- noticed questions versus objections,
- adjusted the conversation based on the prospect's responses.

## 9. Questioning

Evaluate whether the caller asked useful questions.

Strong questions may include:

- "Are you familiar with Frost & Sullivan and what we do?"
- "Would you be the right person to discuss this with?"
- "Who would you recommend I speak with?"
- "Is it mainly a timing issue, or are you still unsure what the conversation would cover?"

Avoid traditional sales qualification questions unless directly relevant.

## 10. Objection Handling

Evaluate whether the caller used a calm, structured response.

Preferred framework:

Acknowledge
→ Clarify
→ Normalize / Reassure
→ Explain
→ Connect to Next Step

Do not reward arguing, pressure, or excessive persistence.

## 11. Meeting Framing

Evaluate whether the caller clearly explained why the next meeting is worth the prospect's time.

### 15–20 Minute Overview

Should be framed as:

- an overview of Frost & Sullivan,
- why the company surfaced in the research,
- explanation of Best Practices Recognition,
- explanation of the evaluation process,
- opportunity to ask questions.

### 60-Minute Analyst Briefing

Should be framed as a deeper conversation around:

- strategy,
- innovation,
- growth,
- customer value,
- market leadership,
- differentiation,
- relevant evaluation areas.

## 12. Scheduling

Evaluate whether the caller made scheduling easy.

Preferred options include:

- sending a Microsoft Bookings link,
- offering two possible dates,
- asking morning or afternoon,
- suggesting a specific time.

Strong example:

"Rather than go back and forth on schedules, I'll send you a Microsoft Bookings link so you can choose whatever date and time works best for you."

Avoid unnecessary scheduling friction.

## 13. Accuracy

Evaluate whether the caller avoided unsupported claims.

The caller should not invent:

- research findings,
- analyst involvement,
- category,
- competitors,
- deadlines,
- criteria,
- pricing,
- licensing terms,
- confidentiality rules,
- selection status.

If uncertain, the caller should say:

"I don't want to give you the wrong information. Let me confirm the exact details rather than guess."

## 14. Call Control

Evaluate whether the caller kept the conversation moving without sounding pushy.

Strong call control includes:

- concise explanations,
- timely questions,
- clear transitions,
- appropriate meeting asks,
- knowing when to stop pushing.

## Overall Score

Provide an overall score from 1 to 10.

The score should reflect the quality of the entire conversation, not just whether a meeting was booked.
