
# Call Review Checklist

## Purpose

Use this checklist immediately after a Frost & Sullivan Best Practices Recognition practice call or live-call review.

The checklist is designed to help identify:

- what worked,
- where the call lost momentum,
- whether the research positioning was clear,
- whether the meeting ask matched the situation,
- whether the caller maintained credibility.

The goal is not to grade every sentence.

The goal is to identify the few moments that most influenced the outcome.

---

## 1. Opening

Check:

- Did the caller clearly identify themselves?
- Did they identify Frost & Sullivan?
- Did the opening sound conversational rather than scripted?
- Did the caller use a natural greeting?
- Did the caller avoid apologetic language?
- Did the caller avoid sounding promotional?

Strong example:

"Hi [NAME], this is [CALLER_NAME] with Frost & Sullivan. How are you doing today?"

---

## 2. Named Analyst Frame

Check:

- Did the caller identify [ANALYST_NAME]?
- Was the analyst positioned as the research lead?
- Was the caller positioned as part of the research team supporting the work?
- Did the caller avoid implying that the analyst personally requested the call unless confirmed?

Preferred:

"I'm reaching out on behalf of my colleague, [ANALYST_NAME], who's leading research in the [INDUSTRY] space."

---

## 3. Research Positioning

Check:

- Did the caller explain that the outreach originated from industry research?
- Did the caller avoid leading with the award?
- Did the caller explain why [COMPANY] is relevant?
- Did the caller distinguish nomination from final recognition?

Preferred:

"[COMPANY] came to our attention during that work and has been nominated for evaluation through our Best Practices Recognition program."

---

## 4. Relevance

Check:

- Did the caller give the prospect a clear reason to care?
- Was any company-specific information verified?
- Did the caller connect the outreach to strategy, innovation, customer value, growth, differentiation, or market leadership where appropriate?
- Did the caller avoid generic flattery?

---

## 5. Familiarity Check

Check:

- Did the caller ask whether the prospect is familiar with Frost & Sullivan?
- Did they adapt based on the answer?
- Did they avoid over-explaining to someone already familiar?
- Did they give enough context to someone unfamiliar?

Preferred:

"Are you familiar with Frost & Sullivan and what we do?"

---

## 6. Conversational Tone

Check:

- Did the caller sound natural?
- Did they listen?
- Did they leave room for the prospect to respond?
- Did they avoid long monologues?
- Did they avoid reading a script word-for-word?
- Did the pace sound controlled and confident?

---

## 7. Best Practices Recognition Explanation

Check:

- Was the program explained clearly?
- Was the explanation concise?
- Did the caller avoid implying guaranteed recognition?
- Did the caller avoid turning the conversation into an award pitch?

Preferred:

"Based on that research, [COMPANY] has been nominated for evaluation through our Best Practices Recognition program, which is our way of recognizing companies that are performing well in their respective fields."

---

## 8. Independent Validation

Check:

- Did the caller explain the value of independent third-party validation where relevant?
- Was the explanation natural rather than promotional?
- Did the caller avoid overstating prestige?

Possible language:

"One of the things that makes the program meaningful is the independent, third-party validation."

---

## 9. Listening

Check:

- Did the caller respond to what the prospect actually said?
- Did they interrupt?
- Did they ask unnecessary questions after the prospect had already answered?
- Did they recognize whether the prospect was receptive, skeptical, busy, or uninterested?
- Did they adjust the call accordingly?

---

## 10. Questions

Check:

- Were questions relevant?
- Did the caller distinguish questions from objections?
- Did they avoid turning the call into discovery?
- Did they avoid pain-point selling?
- Did they use questions to clarify role, familiarity, timing, or concern?

---

## 11. Objection Handling

Check:

- Did the caller acknowledge the concern?
- Did they clarify the real objection where needed?
- Did they answer directly?
- Did they avoid becoming defensive?
- Did they keep the response concise?
- Did they bridge naturally back to the next step?

Recommended framework:

Acknowledge  
→ Clarify  
→ Reassure  
→ Explain  
→ Next Step

---

## 12. Meeting Framing

Check:

- Did the caller explain why the next conversation would be useful?
- Did the meeting duration match the call context?
- Was the 15-to-20-minute overview used for newer or less familiar contacts?
- Was the 60-minute analyst briefing reserved for appropriate situations?
- Did the caller avoid asking for too much too soon?

---

## 13. Scheduling

Check:

- Did the caller make scheduling easy?
- Did they offer Microsoft Bookings where appropriate?
- If scheduling live, did they progressively narrow the options?
- Did they avoid vague asks such as "Let me know when you're free"?

Preferred Bookings language:

"I'll send you my Microsoft Bookings link so you can choose whatever time works best for you."

Preferred live scheduling:

"Would Tuesday or Thursday be easier?"

Then:

"Morning or afternoon?"

Then:

"How about 2:00?"

---

## 14. Referral Handling

If the person was not the right contact, check:

- Did the caller recognize that quickly?
- Did they ask who would be more appropriate?
- Did they ask for useful referral information?
- Did they avoid forcing the wrong person into a meeting?

A referral can be a successful outcome.

---

## 15. Soft No Handling

Check:

- Did the caller distinguish a soft no from a hard no?
- Did they clarify the hesitation?
- Did they make only one second ask?
- Did they stop if the prospect declined again?

---

## 16. Hard No Handling

Check:

- Did the caller respect the decline?
- Did they avoid continuing to pitch?
- Did they close professionally?

A hard no should not become a persistence exercise.

---

## 17. Accuracy

Check:

- Did the caller avoid inventing information?
- Did they distinguish nomination from final recognition?
- Did they avoid unconfirmed claims about criteria, category, deadlines, fees, licensing, competitors, or outcomes?
- Did they correct unsupported information if necessary?

Preferred fallback:

"I don't want to give you the wrong information. Let me confirm the exact details rather than guess."

---

## 18. Commercial vs Research Separation

Check:

- Did the caller maintain the distinction between research and evaluation versus later commercial discussions?
- Did they avoid implying pay-to-play?
- Did they avoid making unsupported statements about licensing or promotional options?

---

## 19. Call Control

Check:

- Did the caller guide the conversation without dominating it?
- Did they recover if the call went off track?
- Did they keep the objective clear?
- Did they know when to move forward?
- Did they know when to stop?

---

## 20. Outcome

Possible successful outcomes include:

- 15-to-20-minute overview scheduled,
- 60-minute analyst briefing scheduled,
- Microsoft Bookings link requested or accepted,
- referral obtained,
- appropriate follow-up date established,
- requested information sent,
- correct contact identified.

Do not judge the call only by whether a meeting was booked.

---

# Fast Self-Review

After the call, ask:

1. Did I sound conversational and confident?
2. Did I clearly explain why Frost & Sullivan was calling?
3. Did I position the named analyst correctly?
4. Did I explain why the company was relevant?
5. Did I listen more than I pushed?
6. Did I answer objections accurately?
7. Did I ask for the right next step?
8. Did I make scheduling easy?
9. Did I avoid overstating anything?
10. What is the one thing I should improve on the next call?

---

# Coaching Rule

Do not overwhelm the caller with ten corrections.

Identify:

- the strongest part,
- the biggest weakness,
- the one improvement that would have the greatest impact.
